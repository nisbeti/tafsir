The Attributes of Allah, Exalted is He... When Allah, Glorified and Exalted, speaks about an action that requires the perfection of His attributes, He says ﴾Indeed, We﴿: ﴾Indeed, We have sent down the Reminder, and indeed, We will preserve it﴿ [al-Hijr: 9].

However, when Allah speaks about His divinity alone and His worship alone, He uses the singular pronoun... like His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾Indeed, I am Allah; there is no deity except Me. So worship Me and establish prayer for My remembrance﴿ [Ta-Ha: 14].

He does not say "worship Us." Thus, in every action where Allah, Exalted is He, uses the plural of majesty, and in every command related to worship and monotheism, He uses the singular. This is so that we understand that the action of Allah is not merely the result of His power alone, nor His knowledge alone, nor His wisdom alone, nor His mercy alone. Rather, every action of Allah encompasses the complete attributes of absolute perfection of Allah.

The plural of majesty serves to draw our attention to this truth, highlighting for the mind the completeness of attributes in Allah. Because as a human, you may have the ability but lack knowledge, or you may have knowledge but lack ability, and you may have knowledge while wisdom eludes you. Therefore, the completeness of attributes is essential (138).

As for the address when the subject pertains to servitude to Allah, the station of pure monotheism necessitates the singular to establish in the worshiper that the object of worship is one and that the deity is one without partner.

﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿

Al-Jawhari said in the Sahih: The path (sirat) is the way.

The path is the way, and it is any passage between two opposing or facing points, like the banks of a river or the summits of two mountains, or truth and falsehood, misguidance and guidance in Islam, or disbelief and faith. The path is singular; it is neither dual nor plural. It has been metaphorically used in the Holy Qur'an for monotheism, as there is no deity but Allah, transitioning from disbelief to faith ﴾Say, "Indeed, my Lord has guided me to a straight path, a correct religion, the religion of Abraham, inclining toward truth, and he was not of the polytheists"﴿ (al-An'am: 161) ﴾Whomever Allah wills to misguide, He leaves astray, and whomever He wills, He guides to a straight path﴿ (al-An'am: 39) ﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿ (al-Fatiha: 7) ﴾So follow me; I will guide you to a straight path﴿ (Maryam: 43) ﴾And indeed, those who do not believe in the Hereafter are deviating from the path﴿ (al-Mu'minun: 74) (139).

And the path in this verse is a metaphor for the truth that leads one to attain the pleasure of Allah, for that attainment is what Islam seeks. The straight is a participle derived from the verb "to be straight," meaning it is without deviation or turns, and it is the best of paths that is straight, which is the main road.
