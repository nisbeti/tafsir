His name is of two kinds: the first is the attributes of His essence, which He has deserved eternally and continuously, and the second is the attributes of His actions, which He has deserved in what is not eternal but rather contingent. (93)

Imam al-Ghazali said: ﴾The Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate﴿ are two names derived from mercy, and mercy necessitates a recipient of mercy, and there is no recipient of mercy except one who is in need. The one who fulfills the need of the needy without intention, desire, or care for the needy is not called merciful. The one who intends to fulfill the need of the needy but does not fulfill it, if he is able to fulfill it, is not called merciful, for if the intention were complete, he would have fulfilled it. If he is unable, he may be called merciful in consideration of the tenderness he possesses, but this is deficient. True mercy is the bestowal of goodness upon the needy, with the intention of caring for them. General mercy is that which encompasses both the deserving and the undeserving. The mercy of Allah, Exalted is He, is complete and general; its completeness is in that He intended to fulfill the needs of the needy and did fulfill them, and its generality is in that it encompasses both the deserving and the undeserving, and includes the worldly and the hereafter, addressing necessities, needs, and advantages beyond them. He is indeed the Most Compassionate. (94)

As you can see, a servant may possess some meanings of the names, such as the Most Compassionate and the All-Hearing, but the application of the name to him differs from its application to Allah, Exalted is He. The servant's share of the meaning of this name is different from its meaning in relation to Allah, Exalted is He, and the related meanings for the servant are vastly different from what befits the Lord, Glorified and Exalted. The meanings related to the servant remain within the framework of being needy, incapable, and deficient, while the meanings in relation to the Lord, Glorified and Exalted, are within the framework of being self-sufficient, capable, and perfect. Most importantly, the attributes of the Lord, Exalted is He, are divine and lordly attributes, while the attributes of others are within the framework of servitude and submission. It is impossible for this to resemble that in any way, even if the name is the same; the reality is distinct.

In the basmala, there is eloquence: the repetition in the description may be for the purpose of glorifying the described or for emphasis to establish it in the soul, or for comprehensiveness and thoroughness of all meanings of mercy with which one begins when reading the words of Allah, Exalted is He. Among them is the omission related to the ب (ba) in بسم, which has been mentioned before. The omission is said to be for the sake of brevity in expression, and there is a benefit in its omission, as it is a place where only the mention of Allah, Exalted is He, should be presented. If the action were mentioned, which does not exist without its doer, the mention of Allah would not be prioritized.
