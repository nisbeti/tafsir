Indeed, the blessings are countless and immeasurable. You praise Him for these blessings that you have received by the mercy of Allah, Exalted is He, in His Lordship, for Lordship contains no harshness as much as it contains mercy. How could it be otherwise when the attribute of Lordship appears in the opening chapter (Al-Fatiha) between the Most Merciful (Ar-Rahman) and the Most Compassionate (Ar-Rahim) in the basmala, and the Most Merciful (Ar-Rahman) and the Most Compassionate (Ar-Rahim) in the body of the surah?

And Allah, Exalted is He, is the Lord of both the believer and the disbeliever; He is the One who called them all into existence. Therefore, He grants them blessings out of His mercy, not based on what they deserve. The sun rises on both the believer and the disbeliever, and its rays are not withheld from the disbeliever to be given only to the believer. Rain falls on those who worship Allah and on those who worship idols besides Allah, and the air is breathed by the one who says there is no god but Allah and by the one who does not say it (122). This is the provision of Lordship in this worldly life. As for the provision of Divinity, whoever worships Allah sincerely will succeed in the Hereafter, while whoever associates partners with Him will have his deeds nullified, and he will be among the losers in the Hereafter.

Sayed Qutb said: ﴾The Most Merciful (Ar-Rahman) and the Most Compassionate (Ar-Rahim)﴿: This attribute, which encompasses all meanings, states, and fields of mercy, is repeated here in the core of the surah, in an independent verse, to affirm the prominent characteristic of that comprehensive Lordship and to establish the pillars of the permanent connection between the Lord and His subjects, and between the Creator and His creations. It is the connection of mercy and care that evokes praise and commendation; it is the connection that is based on reassurance and pulses with affection. Praise is the innate response to the fresh mercy (123).

﴾The Most Merciful (Ar-Rahman) and the Most Compassionate (Ar-Rahim)﴿, along with His saying ﴾Lord of the worlds (Rabb al-Alamin)﴿, are attributes of praise because what precedes them (Allah) is a proper noun that does not allow for shared naming, thus it is specified (124). He began first with the description of Lordship; if Lord means master, or owner, or deity, it is an active attribute for the one described by it, governing the governed, the owned, and the worshipped as He wills for good and evil (125). Thus, it is appropriate for this description to be accompanied by the attributes of mercy and compassion so that the servant's hope for forgiveness may expand if he stumbles, and his hope may strengthen if he falters. It is not correct for Lord to mean the established or the companion (and these are among its linguistic meanings) due to the impossibility of attributing it to the worlds.
