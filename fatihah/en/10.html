The surah begins with ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment﴿, and all of this is in the style of addressing with the pronoun of the absent, then it shifts to direct address, that is, with the pronoun of the present addressee, in His saying ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿. If we were to measure this against the context of the initial verses, it would have been more appropriate to say "We worship You and we seek help from You." So why did the Exalted One not say this? One of the rhetorical devices is "iltifat" (23), which is the transition of speech from addressing with the pronoun of the addressee or the absent or the speaker to another form. This alone is considered a mark of eloquence, and Ibn Jinni called it the courage of the Arabic language because this change renews the listener's engagement. If a subtle consideration that suits the transition is added, it becomes one of the rhetorical devices and is counted among the treasures by the eloquent Arabs. There are countless instances of it in the Qur'an with precise appropriateness in the transition; the general benefit of iltifat is to invigorate the listener's activity and stimulate the mind to listen and pay attention. As for the benefit required by the context, when the eloquent speaker shifts, this transition has a benefit beyond the general one. An example of this is: ﴾He it is who causes you to travel on land and sea; until when you are in the ships and they sail with them with a good wind, and they rejoice in it, there comes a stormy wind, and the waves come to them from every place, and they think they are surrounded, they call upon Allah, making religion pure for Him. If You save us from this, we will surely be among the grateful﴿ (22) (Yunus). He did not say "and we sailed with you," as there is a shift here because when they boarded the sea and the ships sailed with them, they became absent and were no longer being addressed (24). (23) Iltifat: It is the transition in speech from one pronoun to another. For example, you address someone with the pronoun of the absent (he) and then shift the speech using the pronoun of the addressee (you). It is a custom among the Arabs to address the present and then shift the address to the absent, or to address the absent and then shift it to the present. Also, it is part of iltifat to address the addressee and then return the address to someone else, such as: ﴾But if they do not respond to you﴿, where the address is to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and then he says to the disbelievers: ﴾Know that what has been revealed is by the knowledge of Allah﴿. This is indicated by His saying: ﴾So are you Muslims?﴿ The secret of its beauty is the stimulation of the mind to question why it has shifted from one address to another, and to draw attention to the importance of the place where the pronoun differs, and to alleviate boredom from the monotony of speech in a single style.

(24) The text presents us with a vivid image of this human being who is heedless of his Lord, content with the worldly life. The first thing we notice is this shift at the beginning "He causes you to travel, you were," then it shifts to speaking with the pronoun of the absent ﴾and they sailed with them﴿. As soon as a person flows in the torrent of life, he becomes increasingly distant from his Lord (flowing). Here, he boarded the sea, enjoying the blessings of Allah, who prepared the land for him to dwell in and made the sea subservient to him. Boarding the sea may be in search of fish or sustenance, or traveling across the sea in search of comfort and ease of living. Then, feel with me the shadows cast by ﴾and they sailed with them with a good wind, and they rejoiced in it﴿. The ship sails swiftly towards a world of comfort, joy, and ease, and the wind is favorable, and the sea embraces the ship like a caring mother. Did you not see how the wind is coupled with the preposition "with," as if to say that the sea pulls the ship by its hand out of excessive fear for it? This instills a sense of tranquility in the soul – and this is a parable of the worldly life that they were pleased with and found security in, so they became heedless of the meeting with their Lord and His signs. I say: that state leaves the imagination to wander in the realm of dreams, and while they are in such a state, a stormy wind strikes their ship, casting them from a world of comfort, joy, and tranquility into a world of anxiety, fear, and turmoil. A wind that roars, making the waves like mountains, causing the ships to become like feathers in the raging wind, the ships are about to capsize, their hearts are torn, and they cling to the sides of the ship. The waves strike them harshly in their faces, throwing them against the ship's rudder, they are surrounded, death comes to them from every direction. Thus, the iltifat here has a remarkable effect in the eloquence of the context, for after a person is enjoying the care of Allah in this worldly life ﴾He causes you to travel﴿, he forgets the favor of the One who cared for him and becomes increasingly distant from Him ﴾and they sailed with them﴿!!
