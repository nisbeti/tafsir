If a person unjustly kills another person, this act occurs without the pleasure of Allah, Exalted is He, but it does not happen by compulsion from Him; it does not occur against His will. Thus, while it falls within the scope of His will, since it does not happen in accordance with what pleases Him, this indicates that the doer was not compelled but had a choice. The confusion arises from the assumption that every will is commanded and pleasing. Therefore, it is necessary to distinguish between will, which is the basis for compulsion, and command (which leads to compulsion), and pleasure. An example of this is His saying in Surah Al-An'am 148: ﴾Those who associate others with Allah will say, "If Allah had willed, we would not have associated anything, nor would our forefathers, nor would we have prohibited anything." Thus did those before them deny until they tasted Our punishment. Say, "Do you have any knowledge that you can produce for us? You follow not except assumption, and you are not but falsifying." (148) Say, "To Allah belongs the decisive argument. If He had willed, He would have guided you all." (149)﴿

Know that He, Exalted is He, reported the explicit statement of the compulsionists (al-Mujbira) (147), which is their saying: "If Allah had willed for us not to associate, we would not have associated." He mentioned this statement in the context of blame and condemnation, thus this doctrine must be deemed blameworthy and false. He followed it with the statement ﴾And you are not but falsifying﴿, meaning you are lying, thus refuting their argument and invalidating their claim.

The second point is that you say: If our actions were contrary to the will of Allah, we would have overcome Allah and opposed Him, which would imply that He is incapable and weak, and this would undermine His divinity.

Allah, Exalted is He, responded to this by stating that incapacity and weakness only follow if I were unable to compel them to believe and obey through coercion and force, and I am capable of that, which is the meaning of His saying: ﴾And if He had willed, He would have guided you all﴿. However, I do not compel you to believe and obey through coercion and force, because that would nullify the wisdom required from the obligation. Thus, it is established through this explanation that what they say, that if we were to act contrary to the will of Allah, it would necessitate that He is incapable and weak, is false.

The essence of this argument is that they argue against the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) by saying that if what they are upon were not with the pleasure of Allah, He would have diverted them from it and would not have facilitated it for them. They say this in an attempt to refute the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and invalidate His judgment upon them regarding misguidance. This is a fallacy of those with misguided intellects who do not differentiate between Allah's actions in creation and decree and the preservation of the laws of existence, which we refer to as will and intention, and His actions in commanding and prohibiting, which we refer to as pleasure.

(147) The compulsionists (al-Mujbira) are a sect that claims that a person is like a feather in the wind, having no choice; they deny any real action by the servant and attribute it entirely to the Lord. There are different types of compulsionists: the absolute compulsionists who do not affirm any action or ability to act at all for the servant, and the moderate compulsionists who affirm a non-effective ability for the servant. As for those who affirm that the acquired ability has some effect in the mind and call that acquisition, they are not considered compulsionists. The Mu'tazila label those who do not affirm the acquired ability in creation and events as compulsionists, and they consider the Ahl al-Sunnah, the Ash'aris, as compulsionists.
