And Allah is the origin of the deity, and its counterpart is the people, whose origin is the human beings. The deity (ilāh) is one of the names of the categories that applies to every object of worship, whether true or false, but it has predominantly come to refer to the object of worship that is true. As for Allah, with the hamza omitted, it is specific to the object of worship that is true and is not applied to anything else. It is a name and not an attribute, because you can describe it but you cannot describe it with it; you do not say "something is a deity" just as you do not say "something is a man." You say, "Allah is One, the Eternal." And because His attributes, Exalted is He, must have a subject upon which they are applied, if all of them were made attributes, they would remain attributes that do not apply to a named subject, and this is not permissible.(52)

They differed regarding this name whether it is derived or a proper name for the essence. Many scholars inclined towards the former. They disagreed about its derivation and origin; Sibawayh narrated from al-Khalil that its origin is ilah, like fi'al; so the definite article was introduced in place of the hamzah. Sibawayh said: "Like the people, its origin is anas." Abu Hayyan al-Tawhidi said: "The hamzah was deleted arbitrarily, as it was said that the people’s origin is anas, or it was deleted for the sake of transmission and it was required with the assimilation, and both statements are rare."(53)

It is said: The root of the word is "lah," and upon it the definite article "al" was added for the purpose of glorification, and this is the choice of Sibawayh. And it was recited:

O son of your uncle, you have not preferred me in lineage over yourself, nor are you more religious than I am, so do not humiliate me(54).

Thus is the narration: " فتخزوني" (fatakhzuni), with the khāʾ (خ) that has a dot, and its meaning is: "to govern me." Abu Hayyan al-Tawhidi said: It is said to be derived and its root is said to be qāf (ق), lām (ل), and hāʾ (ه) from "lāh" (لاه) meaning "to rise." It is said that is why the sun is called "ilāh" (إله) with both a broken and a opened hamzah (همزة). It is said to be lām (ل), wāw (و), and hāʾ (ه) from "lāh" (لاه) meaning "to be veiled or illuminated," and its form at that time is either "faʿal" (فعل) or "faʿil" (فعل). It is said that the alif (ا) is extra and its root is hamzah (همزة) and lām (ل) from "alāh" (أله) meaning "to be frightened," as said by Ibn Ishaq, or "to be perplexed," as said by Abu Umar, or "to be tranquil," as said by al-Mubarrad(55).

Al-Kisai and al-Farra' said: The meaning of ﴾In the name of Allah﴿ is "In the name of the God"; they omitted the hamza and merged the first lam into the second, making them a doubled lam. Al-Zamakhshari said: As for the lam, it is to separate it from the lam of initiation, and as for the ba, it is because it is necessary for the letter and the genitive case, and "name" is one of the ten names that have their beginnings based on a consonant. When they pronounce it at the beginning, they add a hamza, so that their initiation does not start with a consonant, since their habit is to begin with a vowel and pause on a consonant, to preserve their language from any defect or ugliness, and to place it at the utmost level of precision and eloquence. Some of them did not add it and sufficed with moving the consonant, saying: "I name" and "name."
