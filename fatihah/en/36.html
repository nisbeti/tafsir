If he had said "I praise Allah," it would not have conveyed this meaning(111), and it is one of the sources that govern actions that are implied and hardly mentioned alongside it. The definition for the genus (in: ﴾al-hamd﴿) is for the essence as it is, without the restriction of its commonality, and its meaning is: an indication of what everyone knows, that praise is what? Or for totality, as all praise in reality is for Allah, for there is no good except that He is its source, whether directly or indirectly. As He said: ﴾And whatever blessing you have, it is from Allah﴿. It is also said to be for the specific reference, and the specific reference is His praise (Exalted is He) of Himself in His eternity.

Shuqayq ibn Ibrahim said in the interpretation of ﴾All praise is due to Allah﴿: It is on three levels: the first is that when Allah gives you something, you recognize who has given it to you, the second is that you are content with what He has given you, and the third is that as long as His power is within your body, you do not disobey Him. These are the conditions of praise.(112)

And it is astonishing that when you thank a person for their good deed, you spend hours and hours... counting words of gratitude and praise, deleting and adding, and seeking the opinions of others. Until you arrive at a poem or a speech filled with praise and thanks. However, Allah, Exalted is He, with His might and greatness, has blessings that are countless and unnumbered; He has taught us to thank Him with two words: "All praise is due to Allah."(113).
