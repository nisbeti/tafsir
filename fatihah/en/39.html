He described him as: "I cannot enumerate Your praises; You are as You have praised Yourself," narrated by Muslim, Abu Dawood, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, and Ahmad.

The Exalted said: ﴾the worlds﴿. The scholars of interpretation have differed greatly regarding "the worlds." Qatadah said: "The worlds" is the plural of "world," which refers to everything that exists besides Allah, and it does not have a singular form like "group" or "people." It was said: "The people of every era are a world," as stated by al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl, due to His saying: ﴾Do you approach the males among the worlds?﴿ [al-Shu'ara: 165], meaning among the people.

Ibn Abbas said: "The worlds are the jinn and mankind," as evidenced by His saying: ﴾to be a warner to the worlds﴿ [al-Furqan: 1], and He was not a warner to the animals. Al-Farra' and Abu Ubaidah said: "The world refers to those who possess intellect, and animals are not called 'worlds,' because this plural is specifically for those who have understanding." Ibn Abbas also said: "Every living being that moves on the face of the earth."

I (al-Qurtubi) say: The first opinion is the most correct of these opinions, (that is, "the worlds" is the plural of "world," which refers to everything that exists besides Allah) because it encompasses every creature and existence, as evidenced by His saying: ﴾Pharaoh said, 'And what is the Lord of the worlds?' He said, 'The Lord of the heavens and the earth and whatever is between them﴿ [al-Shu'ara: 23]. It is derived from 'alam and 'alama because it indicates its Creator. Thus, al-Zajjaj said: "The world is everything that Allah created in this world and the Hereafter." Al-Khalil said: "The 'alam, 'alama, and ma'lam are what indicate a thing; thus, the world indicates that it has a Creator and a Planner, and this is clear. (120).

Ibn Faris said: (ʿilm) with the letters ʿayn, lam, and mim is a correct and singular root that indicates a mark by which something is distinguished from another, from which comes 'alama, and from this comes 'alamin, as every kind of creation is in itself a mark and a sign. Some said: "The world is named for its gathering." Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿ [al-An'am: 45, al-Saffat: 182], and they said: "All creatures." They recited: "I have neither seen nor heard of anyone like them in the worlds."

And he said regarding the world: * "A great leader of this world." *

What the poet said regarding this indicating gathering and assembly is not far-fetched, as they call the 'alam, and it is said that it is the sea, and it is said that it is the well with abundant water. (121)

And the definite article (al) in "the worlds" indicates comprehensiveness, and what we see is that "the worlds" is the plural of "world," and the world is everything that exists besides Allah. The world can be pluralized as 'awalim and 'alamin; thus, the Lord of the worlds means the Lord of the worlds, and "the worlds" is a masculine plural. If it is said that this plural is for the rational beings, then it becomes the Lord of the rational beings.
