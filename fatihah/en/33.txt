The "praise" is different from "commendation," for the latter is something that is commanded, as the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever does not praise people has not praised Allah."
From His saying, ﴾All praise is due to Allah﴿, we learn that Allah is alive, possessing the most beautiful attributes and excellent actions, so we praise Him for His attributes and for His actions and bounties. If the praise were solely for Allah, it would not convey any of that, thus the choice of "praise" is preferable to the choice of "commendation" (101).
Praise is the expression and calling out of the beautiful, whether it be a blessing or otherwise, with love and reverence; you say: "I praised the man for his bounty," and "I praised him for his lineage and bravery." As for gratitude, it is specifically for the blessing and is expressed through the heart, tongue, and limbs. He said:
"The blessings have benefited you from me in three ways: my hand, my tongue, and my concealed conscience."
And my gratitude was not sufficient for your generosity, but I endeavored in my effort to honor you.
This means that my glorification of you was not sufficient to fulfill the right of your gift, but I intended through my striving in honoring you a means. He clarified this by saying: "Your blessings upon me are manifested through my hand, my tongue, and my heart; they are yours along with their actions." The noble master said: This is a moral testimony that gratitude applies to the actions of the three resources, and it indicates that he made them a reward for the blessing. Everything that is a reward for a blessing, by convention, is called gratitude in language. It is as if he said: "Your blessings have increased upon me, thus it is obligatory to fulfill the types of gratitude to you," and he emphasized this until he made their resources a possession for them.
Praise is by the tongue alone; it is one of the branches of gratitude, and from it is his saying (peace be upon him): "Praise is the head of gratitude; no servant has truly thanked Allah who has not praised Him" (102). He made it the head of gratitude because mentioning the blessing with the tongue and praising its giver is more widespread and indicative of its status than belief and the etiquette of the limbs. Praise is the opposite of blame, and gratitude is the opposite of ingratitude (103).
It has been mentioned in Lisan al-Arab that "praise and gratitude are close in meaning, but praise is broader than gratitude because you praise a person for their inherent qualities and for their gifts, but you do not thank them for their qualities. Thus, the choice of praise is also preferable to gratitude because it is broader; you commend Him for His blessings that reach you and all of creation, and you commend Him for His inherent beautiful attributes, even if none of them pertain to you. Therefore, the choice of praise is preferable to commendation and gratitude."
It has been said that praise and gratitude are synonymous, or that praise is broader and gratitude is a commendation for Allah (Exalted is He) for His actions, while praise is a commendation for His attributes.
