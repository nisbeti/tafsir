In the name of Allah, the ب (ba) is for seeking assistance(46), meaning I read in the name of Allah, or I perform ablution seeking His help, while being optimistic and seeking blessings(47).

The term "name" is derived from "wasm," which means a mark, because a name is a mark for that which it is designated. This is the view of the Kufans. As for the Basrans and most grammarians, they hold that the name is derived from "sama," meaning elevation and height, because it elevates the named entity, distinguishing it from others. Al-Nasafi said: "Its derivation is from 'sama,' which is elevation, because naming is an exaltation of the named and a glorification of its mention." The view of the Kufans is more apparent in terms of meaning, but the inflection and pluralization of the name strengthen the opinion of the Basrans, as it is diminutive form is "sumayy," and if it were from "sima," its root would be "wasm," and its plural would be "awsam," and its diminutive would be "wusaym," because pluralization and diminutive forms return things to their origins.(48)

The name is the term that denotes, by convention, something that exists in reality if it is tangible, and in the minds if it is abstract, without being affected in its structure by time. Its meaning is the named thing(49)

Allah

And ﴾Allah﴿ is a proper noun for "the Lord" (Rabb), Exalted is He, specific to Him, and it is not permissible to name anyone else with it. Sibawayh said, "It is the most well-known of the proper nouns." It is the root of His beautiful names and indicates all of them, as well as His supreme attributes. In fact, it has been said that it is the Greatest Name.(50)

The definite article (al) is used for a specific person or type, for presence, for hinting at a quality, for predominance, and it is also used in a connected manner.(51)

(46) The preposition "bi" (ب) comes with several meanings: for attachment, assistance, oath, cause, state, circumstantial, and transfer. Attachment is literal, as in "I wiped my head" and metaphorical, as in "I passed by Zayd." Assistance is exemplified by "I slaughtered with the knife." The cause is illustrated by ﴾So, due to the wrongdoing of those who were Jews, We prohibited them from good things﴿. The oath is shown in "By Allah, he stood up." The state is indicated by "Zayd came with his clothes." The circumstantial meaning is "Zayd is in Basra." The transfer is "I stood with Zayd." It can also be extra for emphasis: "I drank with the water of the sea." The substitution is "I wish I had a people like them," meaning instead of them. The comparison is "I bought the horse for a thousand." The exceeding is indicated by ﴾And the Day the heaven will be split open with clouds﴿, meaning from the clouds. The superiority is shown in ﴾Whoever you trust with a qintar will return it to you﴿. Some have referred to the state as companionship and added that it can also indicate reasoning. They referred to assistance as the cause and to the state as meaning "with," in agreement with the meaning of "lam" (ل). This is explained in the book Al-Bahr Al-Muhit by Abu Hayyan Al-Tawhidi.

It is also the most specific of the names, as no one applies it to anyone else, neither in reality nor in metaphor. For these two reasons, it seems that this name is the greatest of the names. End.

(51) The definite article (al) is for reference to a specific person or category, for presence, and for indicating a characteristic. (The "lam" for indicating a characteristic is like in "al-hasan" and "hasan," and "ibn al-mughafil" and "ibn Mughafil," and so on; this "lam" is called the "lam" for indicating a characteristic here.) It is also for predominance. (The "al" in "Allah" if we say its origin is "al-ilah," is said to be for predominance, as "ilāh" applies to the worshipped one in truth and falsehood, while "Allah" applies only to the worshipped one in truth, thus it is like "al-najm" for "al-thurayya," and "al-bayt" for "al-kabah.") And it can be relative. So, for reference to a specific person: "the boy came," and for category: "I watered the water," and for presence (the "lam" of presence like in ﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion﴿ (al-Ma'idah: 3) and "this man came to me."): "I went out and saw the lion," and for indicating a characteristic: "al-Harith," and for predominance: "al-Dabaran." And it can be necessary or non-necessary; the necessary is like "now," and the non-necessary is like "far removed is the age from its captive," as explained in Tafsir al-Bahr al-Muhit.
