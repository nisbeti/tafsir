And kingship is the domination and authority over those from whom obedience can be obtained, and this can be through merit or without merit. (129).
The scholars have differed on which is more eloquent: "King" (Malik) or "Owner" (Malik)? Both readings are narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) as well as from Abu Bakr and Umar. Al-Tirmidhi mentioned them, and it was said that "King" (Malik) is more general and more eloquent than "Owner" (Malik) because every king is an owner, but not every owner is a king. This is because the kingship is binding on the owner in his ownership, such that he cannot act except in accordance with the management of kingship, as stated by Abu Ubaidah and Al-Mubarrad. It was also said that "Owner" (Malik) is more eloquent because he is the owner of people and others, thus the owner has greater authority and is more significant, as he is responsible for implementing the laws of Sharia, and he has the additional aspect of ownership.
Abu Hatim said: "Indeed, Malik is more eloquent in praising the Creator than 'Malik', and 'Malik' is more eloquent in praising the created beings than Malik. The difference between them is that the owner (malik) among the created beings may not be a king (malik), and when Allah, Exalted is He, is an owner, He is a king. This opinion was chosen by the judge Abu Bakr ibn al-Arabi(130). And we should note that the owner may be a king and may not be, and the king may be an owner and may not be."
Ibn Faris said: "(Religion) is a single root to which all its branches return, consisting of the letters dāl, yā, and nūn. It is a type of submission and humility. Thus, religion (dīn) means obedience; it is said, 'He submitted to him (dāna lahu), he obeyed (yadīn dīnan)' when he accompanied and submitted and obeyed. A people are dīn, meaning they are obedient and submissive." The poet said:
* And the people were except for us in religion *
And the city is as if it is a place of action, named so because obedience to those in authority is established therein. As for His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾What was it that he should take his brother in the religion of the king?﴿ [Yusuf 76], it is said: in his obedience, and it is said in his judgment. From it: ﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿ [al-Fatiha 4], meaning the Day of Judgment. Some have said: the day of reckoning and recompense. Whichever it may be, it is a matter to which one submits. Abu Zayd said: The religion (din) of a man is what he is compelled to follow when he is burdened with what he dislikes. On the Day of Judgment, it is the day of submission to Allah, Exalted is He, and the day when the truth becomes evident to all creatures that Allah, Exalted is He, is the Sovereign, the Judge, who governs people with His commands and prohibitions. Whoever obeys Him succeeds, and whoever disobeys Him is held accountable. It is the day of reckoning, the day of recompense, and the day of religion itself, the day of faith, the day of the emergence and triumph of the true faith. Therefore, we say there is no word that can replace the word "religion."(131)
Belief in the Day of Judgment is a fundamental principle of Islamic creed that holds significant value in directing the attention and hearts of people towards another world beyond this earthly one, so that they are not overwhelmed by the necessities of this world. At that point, they possess the ability to rise above these necessities. They are not consumed by anxiety over achieving the reward for their efforts during their lifetime.
