Its meanings are four: the deity, the lord, the owner, and the reformer, all of which are applicable in ﴾Lord of the worlds﴿, but the most likely meaning is: the deity due to its specificity to Allah, Exalted is He (115). Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi said: The lord is the master, the owner, the stable, the worshipped, and the reformer, and some have added the meaning of the companion, citing the saying:
"Then the lord of the dogs approached him with his white hand, the tips of their feathers torn" (116).
The lord is the owner, and from this is the saying of Safwan to Abu Sufyan: "For a man from Quraysh to be my lord is more beloved to me than for a man from Hawazin to be my lord."
You say: He raised him, he nurtures him, he is a lord; and it may be a description by the source for exaggeration, as it is described by justice (117).
Al-Harawi and others said: It is said of one who undertakes the reform of something and completes it: He has raised it, he nurtures it, so he is a lord to it and a nurturer. From this, the rabbaniyoon (those who are devoted to the scriptures) are named for their commitment to the books. In the hadith: "Do you have a blessing that you nurture upon it?" meaning you uphold it and reform it. The lord is the worshipped, and from this is the saying of the poet:
"Is there a lord upon whose head the foxes urinate... indeed, he has been humiliated by those who urinate upon him."
And it is said in the plural: He raised him, he nurtured him, he brought him up, as reported by al-Nahhas. In the Sahih, it is said: So-and-so raised his child, he nurtured him, and he brought him up, meaning he raised him. The one who is raised is the nurtured.
When you add the definite article "the" to "lord," it becomes specific to Allah, Exalted is He, because it indicates a definite reference. If we remove it, it becomes common between Allah and His servants, as it is said: Allah is the lord of the servants and Zayd is the lord of the house.
The act of reform and nurturing encompasses the worlds - that is, all creatures - and Allah, Exalted is He, did not create the universe and then leave it in vain; rather, He acts within it with reform, cares for it, and nurtures it. All worlds and creatures are preserved and attended to by the care of Allah, Lord of the worlds.
Absolute lordship is the dividing line between the clarity of complete and comprehensive monotheism and the obscurity that arises from the lack of clarity of this truth in its decisive form (118). The Noble Qur'an has informed us of peoples who associate others with Allah in His lordship. He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And if you ask them who created the heavens and the earth and subjected the sun and the moon, they will surely say, "Allah." So how are they deluded? (61) Allah extends provision for whom He wills of His servants and restricts it for him. Indeed, Allah is Knowing of all things. (62) And if you ask them who sent down water from the heavens and gave life thereby to the earth after its lifelessness, they will surely say, "Allah." Say, "Praise be to Allah." But most of them do not understand. (63)﴿ (al-Ankabut). These have taken besides Allah lords, believing that they bring them closer to Allah in rank, and that they benefit them.
