Its root is: samu, as evidenced by its derivations: like asma, sami, and sumitu. Its derivation is from السمو (sama), because naming is an elevation of the named and a glorification of its mention(56).
Then it was said: It (meaning Allah) is derived from "walah" when one is bewildered; and walah means the loss of reason. It is said: a bewildered man (rajul walah) and a bewildered woman (imra'a walah) and a bewildered one (walih). Water that is walah is sent forth in the deserts. Thus, Allah, Exalted is He, causes minds to be bewildered and to lose themselves in the realities of His attributes and thoughts in knowing Him. Based on this, the root of "Ilah" is "walah," and the hamza is substituted for the waw as it is substituted in ishah and wushah, and isadah and wisadah; and it has been narrated from al-Khalil.
It has been narrated from al-Dhahak that he said: It is called "Allah" because creation turns to Him in their needs and supplicates to Him in their hardships. It has been mentioned from al-Khalil ibn Ahmad that he said: Because creation turns to Him (with the lam in the accusative) and also turns to Him (with the lam in the genitive), and both are dialects. It was said that it is derived from elevation; for the Arabs would say for everything elevated: lah, and they would say when the sun rises: lahat. It was said that it is derived from alaha (to worship) a man when he worships. And "ta'allaha" (to devote oneself) is derived from that, as in His saying: ﴾And He will leave you and your gods﴿ in this recitation; for Ibn Abbas and others said: and your worship, they said: Thus, the name of Allah is derived from this, so Allah, Exalted is He, means the one intended for worship, and from this is the saying of the monotheists: ﴾There is no deity but Allah﴿, meaning there is no object of worship other than Allah. And "illa" in the phrase means "other than," not "except"(57).
Abu Sulayman al-Khattabi, may Allah have mercy on him, said in what I have been informed: People have differed, is it a proper name or derived? It has been narrated from al-Khalil with two narrations: one of them is that it is a proper name that is not derived, so it is not permissible to omit the alif or the lam from it, as is permissible from ﴾The Most Merciful, The Most Compassionate﴿. And it was narrated from Sibawayh that it is a derived name, so its original form is Ilah like fi'al, so he inserted the alif and the lam instead of the hamza. And others said: Its origin in speech is Ilah, and it is derived from alaha (the man turns to Him) when he seeks refuge in Him from a matter that has befallen him, so he "alahahu" (granted him refuge) and "amānahu" (granted him safety), thus it is called Ilah just as a man is called Imam when he leads the people and they follow him. Then, since it is a name for the Great One, there is nothing like Him, they wanted to magnify it with the definite article which is the alif and lam, because they singled it out with this name without others, so they said: the Ilah, and they found the hamza heavy in a word that they frequently use, and the hamza in the middle of speech has a strong pressure, so they omitted it, and the name became as it was revealed in the Qur'an(58).
Al-Zajjaj said: As for the discussion regarding our saying (Allah), it is in two aspects, in terms of wording and meaning: As for the wording, it is in two sayings: one of them is that its root is Ilah, fi'al, and it is said rather that its root is lah, fa'al, and do not pay attention to what was mentioned in the "Book of the Qur'an," for the correct is what is mentioned here.
