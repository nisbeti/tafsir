Al-Farsi said: "It is only said 'In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful' and the term 'the Most Merciful' comes after the encompassing meaning of 'the Most Gracious' to specify the believers with it in His saying, ﴾And He is Merciful to the believers﴿(79). Its meaning according to the people of language is 'the possessor of mercy' which has no limit after it in mercy, because 'Fa'lan' is a form of exaggeration, and 'Merciful' (Rahim) is 'Fa'il' meaning 'one who does' as they say 'Hearing' (Sami') meaning 'one who hears' and 'Able' (Qadir) meaning 'one who is able'. Al-Azhari said: 'It is not permissible to say 'the Most Gracious' (Rahman) except for Allah, the Exalted, and 'Fa'lan' is a form used to exaggerate in description. So, 'the Most Gracious' (Rahman) is the one whose mercy encompasses everything, and it is not permissible to say 'Rahman' for anyone other than Allah. Al-Azhari narrated from Abu Abbas regarding His saying, ﴾The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿: they were combined because 'the Most Gracious' is Hebrew and 'the Most Merciful' is Arabic."

Ibn Abbas said: They are two gentle names, one of which is gentler than the other; the Most Merciful (al-Rahman) is the gentler, and the Most Compassionate (al-Rahim) is the one who shows affection to His creation through provision(80).

Al-Khattabi said: "This is problematic; for gentleness (raka) has no place in any of the attributes of Allah, Exalted is He." Al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl al-Bajali said: "This is an error from the narrator, for gentleness is not one of the attributes of Allah, Exalted is He, but rather they are two names that are gentle, one being more gentle than the other, and gentleness is one of the attributes of Allah, Mighty and Majestic; the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'Indeed, Allah is gentle and loves gentleness, and He grants through gentleness what He does not grant through harshness.' " (81).

Al-Hasan said: "The Most Merciful (al-Rahman) is a name that cannot be used for anyone other than Allah. However, one may say a merciful man (rajul rahim). Al-Jawhari said: 'The Most Merciful (al-Rahman) and the Merciful (al-Rahim) are two names derived from mercy (rahma), and their counterparts in the language are nadim and nadman, which have the same meaning. It is permissible to repeat the two names if their derivation differs for the purpose of emphasis, as one may say so-and-so is diligent and industrious (jaddu mujidd). However, the name al-Rahman is specific to Allah, Exalted is He, and it is not permissible to use it for anyone else or to describe anyone else with it. Do you not see that He said ﴾Say, 'Call upon Allah or call upon the Most Merciful'﴿? He equated it with the name that no one else shares with Him, and both are forms of exaggeration. Al-Rahman is more intense than al-Rahim, and al-Rahim can be used to describe someone other than Allah, as one may say a merciful man (rajul rahim)(82) and not..."
