Said He, Exalted is He, ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿. He is always present, thus addressed with the address of the present addressee. The speech from the beginning of the Opening (Al-Fatiha) to ﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿ is all praise for Allah, the Exalted, and praise can be given in presence and absence; however, praise in absence is more sincere and appropriate. As for ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿, it is a supplication, and supplication in presence is more appropriate and effective. Thus, praise in absence is more appropriate, and supplication in presence is more appropriate, and worship is performed in the presence and is more appropriate (25).

Another matter regarding the shift in address here is that when a person begins to pray, he usually comes with his mind preoccupied with worldly matters. The prayer gradually leads him towards humility until he reaches the sincerity of worshiping Allah alone and the sincerity of seeking help from Allah alone. His heart has then brought to mind the meeting with his Lord, so it is fitting that he addresses Him with the pronoun of the addressee after having addressed Him with the pronoun of the absent.

Another point is to achieve the benefit of sincerity in servitude and in seeking help, and for this reason, the pronoun in both is that of the addressee.

Why did He choose the word "the path" (as-sirat) instead of "the way" or "the route"? If we observe the linguistic structure of "the path," it is on the pattern of (fa'al with a kasra on the fa), which is one of the patterns indicating comprehensiveness, like a belt, a strap, a cover, and a bedding. This form indicates comprehensiveness, unlike the word "the way," which does not convey the same meaning. "The path" indicates that it is wide and spacious, accommodating all who tread upon it, while the word "way" is on the pattern of fa'il, meaning that which is tread upon, and "route" is on the pattern of fa'il, and we say "the road has become wide" if many people walk upon it, but its form does not indicate comprehensiveness. Thus, the word "the path" indicates comprehensiveness and spaciousness, and this is in the essence of the linguistic structure. (Al-Zamakhshari said in his book Al-Kashaf that "the path" is from "sarat," as if it swallows the routes whenever those who tread upon it walk in it, as if it swallows them due to its vastness).

The conclusion of Surah Al-Fatiha is appropriate for everything mentioned in the Surah from beginning to end. Whoever does not praise Allah, the Exalted, is indeed under His wrath and misguided, and whoever does not believe in the Day of Judgment and that Allah, Exalted is He, is ﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿ and its King, and whoever does not dedicate worship and seeking help to Allah, the Exalted, and does not find guidance to the straight path; they are all under His wrath and misguided (26).

Let us see where ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿ is mentioned in full in the Qur'an: ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿ is at the beginning of Al-Fatiha after the Basmala, meaning the beginning of action; and at the end of Surah As-Saffat after discussing the universe and what it contains until the Day of Resurrection, which is the end of life ﴾Glory be to Your Lord, the Lord of Might, above what they describe﴿.
