"Prayer in which one does not recite the Mother of the Book (Um al-Qur'an) is deficient," three times, incomplete. It was said to Abu Huraira: "We are behind the imam." He said: "Recite it to yourself. For I heard the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) say: "Allah, Exalted is He, said: 'I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into two halves. And for My servant is what he asks. When the servant says: ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿, Allah, Exalted is He, says: 'My servant has praised Me.' And when he says: ﴾The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿, Allah, Exalted is He, says: 'My servant has glorified Me.' And when he says: ﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿, He says: 'My servant has exalted Me (and He said once: 'My servant has entrusted Me').' When he says: ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿, He says: 'This is between Me and My servant, and for My servant is what he asks.' When he says: ﴾Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked [Your] anger or of those who are astray﴿, He says: 'This is for My servant, and for My servant is what he asks.'" And in another narration: "These are for My servant, and for My servant is what he asks(6)."
Surah Al-Fatiha teaches us how to interact with Allah. Its beginning is a praise of Allah, Exalted is He, ﴾الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ العَالَمِين﴿, and its end is a supplication to Allah for guidance ﴾اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ﴿. If we divide the letters of Surah Al-Fatiha, we find that half of its letters are praise (63 letters) from ﴾الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ﴿ to ﴾وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ﴿, and half of its letters are supplication (63 letters) from ﴾اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ﴿ to ﴾وَلاَ الضَّالِّينَ﴿. It is as if it affirms the sacred hadith: "I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into two halves, and My servant will have what he asks for." Glory be to Allah, the Mighty, the Wise, who has decreed all things.
Then Abu Ubaid said: And Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Amwi narrated to us, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, from Umm Salama who said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) would pause in his recitation; ﴾In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Master of the Day of Judgment.﴿ And likewise(7), meaning he would recite the verse and then pause, then recite the one that follows.
Omar ibn Abdulaziz (may Allah be pleased with him) was asked why he pauses after each verse of Surah Al-Fatiha, and he replied, "So that I may enjoy the response of my Lord"(8). Oh Allah, how beautiful it is for this supplication, this prayer, to circulate between the servant and his Lord. The heart indeed becomes dry and shivers from the awe of the moment when the servant stands before his Master, his Lord, and his King, the Lord of the seven heavens and the earths, conversing with Him, and the Lord of the worlds responds to him, bringing him a promise from Allah, the Exalted, that he will have what he has asked for!!
And He is the One capable of granting by a command between the letter "kaf" and "nun": "Be" and it is!!
He has what he asks for when he seeks His help with complete and comprehensive reliance, and he has what he asks for when he requests guidance to his Lord's straight path!
