This surah is about prayer and establishes that congregational prayer is better than individual prayer by twenty-seven times. It contains evidence of the importance of the congregation in Islam in general, such as the community of Muslims under a caliph who governs them by the law of Allah. This is why the year in which Imam Hasan ibn Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) relinquished the caliphate to Muawiya (may Allah be pleased with him) is called the Year of the Congregation. This includes pilgrimage, congregational prayer, zakat, jihad, festivals, and fasting. Additionally, the believers are brothers; if one says "You alone we worship," it would neglect the worship of his fellow believers. However, when we say ﴾إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ﴿ ("You alone we worship"), we mention all the believers, and the speaker also enters the ranks of the believers.
Why is worship coupled with seeking help?
Firstly, it indicates that a person cannot perform the worship of Allah except with His assistance and guidance. It is, therefore, a reminder and declaration that a person cannot do anything without Allah's help, and it is an acknowledgment of human inability to fulfill acts of worship and bear the heavy trust unless Allah assists him in that. Seeking help from Allah is a remedy for human arrogance and pride in not seeking Allah's assistance and an acknowledgment of human weakness.
Why is worship mentioned before seeking help?
Worship is the reason for the creation of jinn and humans ﴾وما خلقت الجن والانس الا ليعبدون﴿ ("And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me") (adh-Dhariyat 56), and seeking help is merely a means to worship, so worship takes precedence. Worship is the right of Allah, while seeking help is a request from His rights, and the right of Allah takes precedence over His requests.(137)
The singular pronoun and the majestic plural in the address of the Lord of the worlds
The Lord of the worlds said ﴾إياك﴿ ("You alone"), and did not say: ﴾إياكم﴿ ("You all"). In other places in the Qur'an, He says: ﴾إنا﴿ ("Indeed, We"). When is the singular pronoun used, and when is the majestic plural used in the address of the Lord of the worlds?
The scholar al-Sharawi (may Allah have mercy on him) said:
Here, we must note that Allah, Exalted is He, when informing us about an action of His, always comes with the majestic plural, which we call the "plural of majesty." We notice that the majestic plural is used by heads of states and kings, who say, "We, so-and-so, have commanded what is to come." It is as if the greatness in a human being has harnessed various talents to implement the decision issued by the head of state. The police, judiciary, state, and armed forces participate in executing the decision if it is a decision of war. Various talents from different groups collaborate to implement the decision. (137) Fadl al-Samarrai, with modifications, in his work "Lamasat Bayaniya," also stated: "Say, 'He is the Most Merciful; we have believed in Him, and upon Him we have relied'" (al-Mulk 29). The belief is presented before the prepositional phrase here because belief is not limited to Allah alone; rather, we must believe in Allah, His messengers, His books, His angels, the Last Day, and divine decree. Therefore, it did not come as "we have believed." However, in reliance, it came as "and upon Him we have relied," not "we relied upon Him," because reliance is confined to Allah, Exalted is He.
