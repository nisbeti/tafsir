In language: praising with beauty with the intention of glorification and veneration, and in customary usage: an act that indicates the glorification of the benefactor due to his being a benefactor. Gratitude (shukr) in language: an act that expresses the glorification of the benefactor, thus it is synonymous with customary praise (hamd).
It is said: Praise (hamd) is more comprehensive because it includes the meaning of gratitude (shukr) and the meaning of commendation. It is broader than gratitude, because praise can be used in place of gratitude, but gratitude cannot be used in place of praise.
It is narrated from Ibn Abbas that he said: "Praise be to Allah is the word of every grateful person." And when Adam (peace be upon him) sneezed, he said: "Praise be to Allah." And Allah said to Noah (peace be upon him): ﴾So say, 'Praise be to Allah who has saved us from the wrongdoing people'﴿. And Ibrahim (peace be upon him) said: ﴾Praise be to Allah who has granted me, in old age, Ishmael and Isaac﴿. And in the story of Dawood and Sulayman (peace be upon them), it is said: ﴾And they said, 'Praise be to Allah who has favored us over many of His believing servants'﴿. And He said to His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): ﴾And say, 'Praise be to Allah who has not taken a son'﴿. And the people of Paradise said: ﴾Praise be to Allah who has removed from us sorrow﴿. ﴾And the last of their supplication will be, 'Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds'﴿. Thus, it is the word of every grateful person.
The correct understanding is that praise (hamd) is the commendation of the praised based on his attributes without any prior favor, while gratitude (shukr) is the commendation of the one who is thanked for the favor he has bestowed. Based on this definition, our scholars have stated: praise is broader than gratitude, because praise encompasses commendation, glorification, and gratitude, while recompense is specific and only applies as a reward for someone who has conferred a favor upon you. Thus, praise is broader in the verse because it exceeds gratitude, and praise is mentioned in the sense of contentment; it is said: "I tested him and praised him," meaning I was pleased with him(104).
Praise is a quality of the heart, which is the belief that the one being praised is gracious, bestowing, and deserving of veneration and reverence.
If a person utters the phrase: "I praise Allah" while his heart is heedless of the meaning of the glorification befitting the majesty of Allah, he is lying because he has informed about himself as being a praiser while he is not. However, if he says "All praise is due to Allah," whether he is heedless or mindful of the meaning of glorification, he is truthful because its meaning is: that all praise is due to Allah and belongs to Him. This meaning is present whether the servant is preoccupied with the meaning of glorification and reverence or not. Thus, it is established that saying "All praise is due to Allah" is preferable to saying "I praise Allah" or "We praise Allah." An analogous case is our saying "There is no deity but Allah," as it does not involve lying, unlike our saying "I bear witness that there is no deity but Allah," because he may be lying in that.
