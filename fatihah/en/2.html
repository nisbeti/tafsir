For a reason, Allah, Exalted is He, made it a fundamental part of the prayer, such that a Muslim recites it in every unit of prayer (rak'ah), and in the noble hadith, "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book (Fatihat al-Kitab) or the Mother of the Book (Umm al-Kitab)." Therefore, this surah requires contemplation on its vocabulary, both in its entirety and in its details.

Surat al-Fatiha is one of the surahs with many names; the author of al-Itqan counted more than twenty titles and descriptions that have been mentioned by the reciters since the time of the predecessors. However, in the authentic Sunnah and what has been transmitted regarding its names, only Fatihat al-Kitab, the seven oft-repeated (al-sab' al-mathani), and Umm al-Qur'an or Umm al-Kitab have been established (3). Thus, let us limit ourselves to explaining these three names.

As for its being called Fatihat al-Kitab, this has been established in the Sunnah in many hadiths, including the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book." The term "Fatiha" is derived from "opening," which means removing a barrier from a desired place of entry. Its formulation implies that its subject is something that removes a barrier. In any case, "Fatiha" is a description attributed to the beginning of the Qur'an and is treated like a generic name, then it was added to the Book, and this compound became a dominant name for this surah (4).

Al-Bukhari reported that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The Mother of the Book is the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an."

As for the seven oft-repeated (5), they are seven verses that are recited repeatedly, and they are the great Qur'an. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) referred to them as such when he said in a narration reported by al-Bukhari: "They are the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an that I have been given."

Yunus narrated to me, he said: Ibn Wahb informed us, he said: Ibn Zayd said regarding His saying: ﴾مَثَانِـيَ﴿ (oft-repeated), it is repeated; Moses was repeated in the Qur'an, as were Salih, Hud, and the Prophets in many places.

And Allah, Exalted is He, named it prayer, as in the sacred hadith: Imam Muslim narrated in his Sahih: Sufyan ibn 'Uyaynah informed us from al-Ala' from his father from Abu Huraira from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) who said: "Whoever prays...
