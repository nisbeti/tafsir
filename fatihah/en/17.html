In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds. The argument of Ibn Abbas is the establishment of the basmala (the phrase "In the name of Allah") with every surah in the mushaf (the written copy of the Qur'an), with the companions being careful not to include anything in the mushaf except the words of Allah, and they said: "What is between the two covers is the word of Allah."

And when you begin the first surah, you say "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful" (Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim) except for Surah al-Bara'ah, and the discussion on that will follow. And when you begin a part of a surah, you have the option according to the majority.

And when you complete a surah and begin another, the reciters differ regarding the basmala (the phrase "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful") and whether to omit it.

As for its ruling in prayer, Malik said: it is disliked in obligatory prayers but not in voluntary ones. Al-Shafi'i said: it is obligatory, and the prayer is invalidated by omitting it; he recites it aloud in the loud prayer and silently in the silent prayer. Abu Hanifa holds the same view, except that he recites it silently in all cases. Malik's evidence is that it is not a verse: what is in the authentic hadith from Anas, who said: "I prayed behind the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman, and they would begin with ﴾Alhamdu lillahi rabb al-alamin﴿" and they did not mention the basmala at all. Al-Shafi'i's evidence is that it is, in his view, a verse: what has been reported in the hadith about reciting it as mentioned earlier(44).

The esteemed professor Abu al-Harith Nur al-Din al-Tamimi, may Allah reward him abundantly, said:

A Muslim cannot differ with another Muslim on any matter of belief as long as it is definitively established, and there is no disagreement between them on its certainty. If there is a disagreement regarding its certainty or probability, then with the occurrence of the disagreement (if the disagreement is justified), it naturally descends from the level of certainty to the level of probability. Muslims have disagreed on whether the Basmala (the phrase "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful") is a verse in Al-Fatiha (the Opening) or not; thus, the mere existence of disagreement regarding the Basmala indicates that it is not a verse. If it were a verse like the saying of Allah, Exalted is He, ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿, the Muslims would not have disagreed about it. They did not disagree on the definitively established beliefs; for example, they did not disagree on Surah Qaf or any of its verses being part of the Noble Qur'an. It has been concluded that Al-Qurtubi (may Allah have mercy on him) said in his Tafsir (exegesis): "The fifth: the correct opinion among these statements is the one of Malik, because the Qur'an is not established by reports of individuals, but rather its path is the definitive, established transmission that there is no disagreement about." Ibn al-Arabi said: "It suffices you that the disagreement of people regarding it indicates that it is not part of the Qur'an, and the Qur'an is not subject to disagreement." The authentic reports that are free from any criticism indicate that the Basmala is not a verse of Al-Fatiha or any other surah except in Surah An-Naml alone.(45)
