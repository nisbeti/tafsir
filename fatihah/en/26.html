It is said that the Most Merciful (al-Rahman) and the Merciful (al-Rahim) may mean the one who is shown mercy. Al-Ma'mar ibn Aqil said: "As for when war bites you, you are indeed with one who is merciful."

And mercy among humans, according to the Arabs, is tenderness of heart and compassion, while the mercy of Allah is His compassion, kindness, and provision. (83)

If you say: What is the meaning of describing Allah, Exalted is He, with mercy, and its meaning is compassion and tenderness, and from it is the womb due to its inclination towards what is within it? I say: It is a metaphor for His bestowal upon His servants, for when a king shows compassion to his subjects and is tender towards them, he bestows upon them his goodness and favors. Just as when he is overtaken by harshness and cruelty, he treats them harshly and withholds his goodness and favors. If you say: Why did He precede what is more profound than the two descriptions with what is lesser, while the norm is to ascend from the lower to the higher, as in their saying: "So-and-so is a learned scholar, a brave hero, and a generous benefactor"? I say: When He said "the Most Merciful," it encompassed the great blessings and their origins, and He followed it with "the Merciful" as a complement and a continuation to include what is subtle and gentle. (84)

The occurrence of two attributes indicating mercy after the word "Lord" indicates to us that mercy is one of the supreme attributes of Allah, Exalted is He, and it implies that the educator should embody mercy and that it should be one of his most prominent attributes, not harshness. The Lord, in all His meanings, should be characterized by mercy, whether He is an educator, a master, or a guardian. Allah, Exalted is He, described His Messenger as merciful. (85)

Ibn al-Mubarak said: "The Most Merciful (al-Rahman) gives when asked, and the Merciful (al-Rahim) becomes angry if not asked." Ibn Majah narrated in his Sunan and al-Tirmidhi in his Jami from Abu Salih from Abu Huraira that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever does not ask Allah, He becomes angry with him," according to the wording of al-Tirmidhi. Ibn Majah said: "Whoever does not call upon Allah, the Exalted, He becomes angry with him." (86)

Abu Hamid al-Ghazali said: The meanings of the names can be conceived such that a servant can embody some of them so that the name applies to him, like "the Merciful," "the All-Knowing," "the Forbearing," "the Patient," "the Appreciative," and others, even though the application of the name to him is in a manner that differs from its application to Allah, Exalted is He. (87)

(84) Al-Kashaf, Mahmoud said: "If you say: Why did He precede what is more profound than the two descriptions with what is lesser... etc.? Ahmad (Ibn al-Munir) said: The norm is to present the lesser of the two descriptions because presenting the higher one followed by the lower one is a form of repetition, as the attainment of the more profound necessitates the attainment of the lesser, so mentioning it afterward is not beneficial. The opposite is not the case, as it ascends from the lesser to the greater with the advantage of the higher not having preceded what it necessitates. Therefore, this arrangement is specific to affirmation. As for negation, it is the opposite; the higher comes first. You say: So-and-so is neither a scholar nor a learned man, and if you reversed it, you would fall into repetition, as negating the lesser from him necessitates negating the higher. All of this is derived from the generality of the lesser and the specificity of the more profound, and affirming the more specific necessitates the affirmation of the more general, while negating the more general necessitates the negation of the more specific." End quote from al-Kashaf, and based on this, we do not agree that "the Most Merciful" is more profound than "the Merciful"! Rather, both are equal in eloquence and emphasis! They are merely attributes of perfection according to the variations of language that convey dimensions of the attribute based on the inflection in each of them.
