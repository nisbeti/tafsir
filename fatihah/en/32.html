Allah has bestowed upon creation sustenance and existence from non-existence, and life and blessings and bounties that cannot be counted. Nothing in His creation is outside of His actual will, so Allah, Exalted is He, is deserving of praise for His divinity and for His lordship. He is also deserving of praise for His mercy, Exalted is He, for His countless bounties with which He shows mercy to His creation and His servants, even to the disbeliever among them, the sinner, and the good-doer. All of creation has not reached the level of worshipping Allah as He deserves, in gratitude for His blessings or even some of them. Thus, He is deserving of praise for His mercy in that He does not punish them for their sins, and that He provides for them while they disobey Him. Likewise, praise is due for His kingship, which includes His description as a King and also as a Owner, deserving of praise. Whether for His kingship in this world (from the position of ﴾Rabb al-‘alamin﴿, we derive His governance, Exalted is He, over this worldly life as a powerful King) or for what is in the Hereafter: ﴾Malik yawm al-din﴿, He is deserving of praise for His absolute justice, in this world and the Hereafter. He is ﴾Malik yawm al-din﴿, who governs it to hold accountable those obligated for what they have done in their worldly lives, leaving no criminal to escape punishment, nor neglecting the good-doer from reward. Thus, He is deserving of praise for His justice, and He is deserving of praise for having established for the obligated a day when they will return to Him for accountability. He did not leave the earth to be governed by the law of the jungle, where the strong takes the rights of the weak without being held accountable for their evil actions. And praise be to Allah for His mercy that encompasses all things. So, praise be to Allah for His divinity, and praise be to Allah for His lordship, and praise be to Allah for His mercy, and praise be to Allah for His kingship and justice.

Then, when He associates lordship (rububiyya) with divinity (uluhiyya), He reassures His servants that the provision of His lordship does not cease even if they err in what deserves praise in the station of His divinity. Their sustenance is continuous, and His benefit to them is ongoing, and His subjugation of what is in the universe for them is persistent unless He wills otherwise, Exalted is He. Thus, He is not to be praised solely for His station of divinity, Exalted is He, so that His lordship is exclusive to His believing servants and not to others. Rather, praise is due to Him, Exalted is He, as the Lord of the worlds as well; He is the Lord of those who praise Him as a deity, and the Lord of those who do not praise Him as a rightful object of worship.

And praise is only for the Living, the Rational.

the most famous distinction made between praise (madح) and commendation (hamd) is that one can commend something inanimate, but one does not praise it. It has been established that commendation is broader than praise; commendation can occur before and after a good deed, while praise can only occur after a good deed. Praise is given for what is present in the qualities or actions that deserve praise; thus, one cannot be praised if their qualities do not warrant it. However, commendation can occur prior to that; one can commend a person who has not done anything praiseworthy or beautiful.
