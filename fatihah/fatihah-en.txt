Commentary on Surat al-Fatiha
Translated from the Arabic: تفسير سورة الفاتحة

In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful
Surah Al-Fatiha
﴾In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful (1) All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds (2) The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful (3) Master of the Day of Judgment (4) You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help (5) Guide us on the Straight Path (6) The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked [Your] anger or of those who are astray (7)﴿
Surah Al-Fatiha: There is a difference of opinion regarding it; the majority hold that it is Meccan, and indeed one of the first revelations of the Qur'an, according to a narration attributed to Ali, Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, and most of the Companions. However, Mujahid claimed it is Medinan, and he was unique in this view to the extent that it was considered a slip on his part. It is said that it was revealed in Mecca when prayer was made obligatory and in Medina when the qibla was changed, to indicate that it is part of the prayer as it was (1).
And the view that it is Meccan is more correct due to His saying: ﴾And We have certainly given you seven of the oft-repeated [verses] and the great Qur'an﴿ [Al-Hijr: 87], and Al-Hijr is Meccan by consensus.
Al-Tirmidhi narrated from Ubayy ibn Ka'b who said that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Allah has not revealed in the Torah or the Gospel anything like Umm al-Qur'an, which is the seven oft-repeated verses, and it is divided between Me and My servant, and My servant will have what he asks." Al-Bukhari narrated from Abu Sa'id al-Mu'alla who said: I was praying in the mosque when the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) called me, but I did not respond. I said: O Messenger of Allah, I was praying. He said: Did Allah not say ﴾Respond to Allah and to the Messenger when he calls you﴿ [Al-Anfal: 24]? - Then he said: "I will teach you a surah that is the greatest surah in the Qur'an before you leave the mosque." Then he took my hand, and when he was about to leave, I said to him: Did you not say you would teach me a surah that is the greatest surah in the Qur'an? He said: ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿; it is the seven oft-repeated verses and the great Qur'an that I have been given (2).

For a reason, Allah, Exalted is He, made it a fundamental part of the prayer, such that a Muslim recites it in every unit of prayer (rak'ah), and in the noble hadith, "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book (Fatihat al-Kitab) or the Mother of the Book (Umm al-Kitab)." Therefore, this surah requires contemplation on its vocabulary, both in its entirety and in its details.
Surat al-Fatiha is one of the surahs with many names; the author of al-Itqan counted more than twenty titles and descriptions that have been mentioned by the reciters since the time of the predecessors. However, in the authentic Sunnah and what has been transmitted regarding its names, only Fatihat al-Kitab, the seven oft-repeated (al-sab' al-mathani), and Umm al-Qur'an or Umm al-Kitab have been established (3). Thus, let us limit ourselves to explaining these three names.
As for its being called Fatihat al-Kitab, this has been established in the Sunnah in many hadiths, including the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book." The term "Fatiha" is derived from "opening," which means removing a barrier from a desired place of entry. Its formulation implies that its subject is something that removes a barrier. In any case, "Fatiha" is a description attributed to the beginning of the Qur'an and is treated like a generic name, then it was added to the Book, and this compound became a dominant name for this surah (4).
Al-Bukhari reported that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The Mother of the Book is the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an."
As for the seven oft-repeated (5), they are seven verses that are recited repeatedly, and they are the great Qur'an. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) referred to them as such when he said in a narration reported by al-Bukhari: "They are the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an that I have been given."
Yunus narrated to me, he said: Ibn Wahb informed us, he said: Ibn Zayd said regarding His saying: ﴾مَثَانِـيَ﴿ (oft-repeated), it is repeated; Moses was repeated in the Qur'an, as were Salih, Hud, and the Prophets in many places.

Surah Al-Fatiha teaches us how to interact with Allah. Its beginning is a praise of Allah, Exalted is He, ﴾الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ العَالَمِين﴿, and its end is a supplication to Allah for guidance ﴾اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ﴿. If we divide the letters of Surah Al-Fatiha, we find that half of its letters are praise (63 letters) from ﴾الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ﴿ to ﴾وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ﴿, and half of its letters are supplication (63 letters) from ﴾اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ﴿ to ﴾وَلاَ الضَّالِّينَ﴿. It is as if it affirms the sacred hadith: "I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant into two halves, and My servant will have what he asks for." Glory be to Allah, the Mighty, the Wise, who has decreed all things.

Oh Allah, how beautiful it is for this supplication, this prayer, to circulate between the servant and his Lord. The heart indeed becomes dry and shivers from the awe of the moment when the servant stands before his Master, his Lord, and his King, the Lord of the seven heavens and the earths, conversing with Him, and the Lord of the worlds responds to him, bringing him a promise from Allah, the Exalted, that he will have what he has asked for!!
And He is the One capable of granting by a command between the letter "kaf" and "nun": "Be" and it is!!
He has what he asks for when he seeks His help with complete and comprehensive reliance, and he has what he asks for when he requests guidance to his Lord's straight path!

The surah begins with the praise of Allah, then His glorification, followed by the declaration of dependence on Him, the submission before Him, the request for assistance from Him, and the seeking of guidance through Him. Thus, half of it is a gathering of praise, and the other half is a gathering of needs. It introduces people to their Lord, with His names and attributes, and informs them of the relationship between them and their Lord: ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿. It guides them to the path of their Lord: the straight path, and it informs them about those who have followed the straight path of their Lord and those who have deviated from it.
The Opening (Al-Fatiha) is called the Great Qur'an because it encompasses all the sciences of the Qur'an, as it includes the praise of Allah, Exalted is He, with descriptions of His perfection and majesty, the command to worship and to be sincere in it, the acknowledgment of one’s inability to perform any of it except with His assistance, and the supplication to Him for guidance to the straight path and for the sufficiency of the conditions of the transgressors, as well as the clarification of the consequences for the deniers.
Al-Fatiha contains the concepts of monotheism (tawhid), worship, reliance (tawakkul), admonition, and reminder(9) and the methodologies of life, and it teaches the question, as it encompasses a range of meanings of the Qur'an from theoretical rulings and practical laws. The sciences include monotheism, attributes, prophecies, admonitions, parables, wisdom, and stories, while the rulings pertain either to the actions of the limbs, which are acts of worship and transactions, or to the actions of the hearts, meaning the intellect, which is the refinement of morals and the etiquette of the Sharia. All of these meanings are included in Al-Fatiha through indication, implication, or entailment. Thus, ﴾All praise is due to Allah﴿ encompasses all attributes of perfection for which Allah deserves to have praise confined to Him, based on what the phrase ﴾All praise is due to Allah﴿ indicates regarding the exclusivity of praise to Him. And ﴾Lord of the worlds﴿ encompasses all attributes of action and creation according to those who affirm them, and ﴾The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿ encompasses the foundations of legislation that relate to mercy for the accountable beings, and ﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿ encompasses the conditions of the Day of Resurrection, and ﴾You alone we worship﴿ gathers the meaning of religion and law, while ﴾And You alone we ask for help﴿ gathers the meaning of sincerity to Allah in actions(10). In the surah, there is an explanation of the conduct of the straight path and insight into the ranks of the blessed and the abodes of the wretched, the former being inferred from the phrase ﴾Guide us﴿; and insight into the ranks of the blessed and the abodes of the wretched is derived from the phrase ﴾Those upon whom You have bestowed favor﴿, etc. In it, there is a promise and a warning, thus both are included(11). The parables and stories are intended for admonition, as well as supplication and praise and teaching the question. These are the general meanings of the Qur'anic text in summary, both in terms of correspondence and entailment. The Virtues of Surah Al-Fatiha
The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "By the One in Whose hand is my soul, nothing has been revealed in the Torah, nor in the Gospel, nor in the Psalms, nor in the Qur'an like it, and it is indeed the seven oft-repeated verses that Allah, Exalted is He, has given me" (14). This is a sound and authentic hadith.
Ibn Abbas said: "While the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was with Gabriel, he heard a sound from above him. Gabriel raised his gaze to the sky and said: 'This is a door that has been opened from the heavens, which has never been opened before.' Then an angel descended from it and came to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and said: 'Rejoice in two lights that you have been given, which no prophet before you has been given: the opening of the Book and the last verses of Surah Al-Baqarah; you will not recite a letter from either of them except that you will be granted it'” (15). Authentic.
Abu Huraira said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever prays a prayer in which he does not recite the Opening of the Book, it is incomplete and not complete" (16).
Ibn al-Arabi said: "We have narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and have transmitted to you, {that he said: Allah, Exalted is He, said: 'O son of Adam, I have revealed to you seven verses, three for Me and three for you, and one that is between Me and you; as for the three that are for Me: ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment﴿ and as for the three that are for you: ﴾Guide us to the straight path, the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked [Your] anger or of those who are astray﴿ and as for the one that is between Me and you: ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿} meaning: from the servant is worship, and from Allah, Exalted is He, is assistance" (17).
And from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him): A group of the companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) set out on a journey, and when they stopped at a tribe of the Arabs, they asked for hospitality, but they refused to host them. The chief of that tribe was bitten by a snake, and they tried everything to help him, but nothing was of benefit. Some of them said: "If you go to those men who have settled among you, perhaps one of them has something." So they went to them and said: "O group, our chief has been bitten, and we have tried everything to help him, but nothing has worked. Does anyone among you have anything?" One of them said: "Yes, by Allah, I am a healer, but by Allah, we asked you for hospitality and you did not host us, so I will not heal him until you give us a reward." They agreed on a flock of sheep, and he went and began to spit and recite: "All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds," until it was as if he had been released from a restraint, and he walked away without any ailment (18).

Some subtleties of Surah Al-Fatiha:
The beginning of the surah ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿, and these are the first words of the Mushaf, which correspond to the last words of Surah An-Nas ﴾From among jinn and mankind﴿. Allah, Exalted is He, began with the worlds and concluded with jinn and mankind, meaning that this book contains guidance for all of Allah's creatures from jinn and mankind, and is not just for humans or for Muslims alone.
The last words of Surah Al-Fatiha are the statement of Allah ﴾Not of those who have evoked [Your] anger or of those who are astray﴿, and Surah Al-Baqarah follows it, discussing those who have evoked anger (the Children of Israel) and how they disobeyed their Lord and Messenger. Surah Al-Imran then speaks about those who are astray (the Christians).
The last words of Surah Al-Fatiha, which are a supplication, are connected to the beginning of Surah Al-Baqarah ﴾Guidance for the righteous﴿, as if ﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿ in Al-Fatiha is the guidance mentioned in Surah Al-Baqarah.
The rules of Tajweed in Surah Al-Fatiha are made easy, and there are none of the difficult rules; this, and Allah knows best, is to facilitate its recitation and memorization for all people, whether they are Arabs or non-Arabs (20).
It contains great principles: the first is the purification from nullification and polytheism as included in ﴾You alone we worship﴿. The second is the abandonment of thoughts of self-sufficiency by disavowing strength and power in the face of His greatness, as included in ﴾And You alone we ask for help﴿. The third is the desire to adorn oneself with guidance and righteousness as included in ﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿. The fourth is the desire to embody good examples as included in ﴾The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor﴿. The fifth is the concern for safety from clear misguidance as included in ﴾Not of those who have evoked [Your] anger﴿. The sixth is the concern for the safety of their thinking from mingling with the doubts of falsehood disguised as truth, which is called misguidance, because misguidance is a mistake of the intended path, as included in ﴾Nor of those who are astray﴿.
The style of Al-Fatiha establishes four rules for the introduction: the first rule is to be concise in the introduction so that the souls of the listeners do not become weary from the long wait for the intended purpose, which is evident in Al-Fatiha, and to set a precedent for speakers so that they do not prolong the introduction lest they be attributed to stammering; for the longer the introduction, the shorter the purpose becomes. This shows the reason for placing it before the long surahs, even though it is a short surah. The second is to indicate the intended purpose, which is called the eloquence of the beginning, as this prepares the listeners to hear the details that will be presented to them, making them ready to receive it if they are among those who are receptive, or to critique and complete it if they are at that level. This indicates the speaker's mastery of the purpose and confidence in the correctness of their opinion on it, thereby alerting the listeners to their awareness, and it is a precedent for speakers to be aware of the purposes of their speech.

The opening (al-Fatiha) is called the Mother of the Qur'an for several reasons. The third reason is that it serves as an introduction composed of concise expressions, as the scholars of rhetoric have clarified when discussing the contexts in which a speaker should be eloquent. The fourth reason is that it begins with praise of Allah.(21).
﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿, why is the worldly life not mentioned? Whether He is the Master or the Sovereign, why does it not say Master of the Day of Judgment and the worldly life? First, it has already said ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿, so He is their Master and Sovereign in this world, and we derive this from the meaning of Lord, which is the owner, and this encompasses the worldly life. ﴾Master of the Day of Judgment﴿ refers to the Master of the Day of recompense, meaning the authority over the preceding days of action - and action occurs in this world - likewise, the root of the word "religion" (din) signifies a type of submission and humility. Thus, religion means obedience; it is said, "He submitted to him" (dan lahu) meaning he accompanied and submitted and obeyed. A people are obedient (qawm din), meaning they are submissive and compliant; all creatures will submit to meet their Lord for reckoning of their deeds, as you reap what you sow. Therefore, the choice of the term "religion" after Allah's statement ﴾The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿ serves to remind humanity of the aspect of severity, submission, and reckoning before the Lord of the worlds, so that they may do good deeds in this world. Ibn Ashur said: When Allah described Himself as ﴾Lord of the worlds, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿, this indicates His complete kindness towards His creations in all their states, and then it serves as a reminder that His actions in the worlds and phases are actions of mercy when considered. The actions of command and prohibition, expressed through legislation aimed at preserving the interests of people, both general and specific, most of those legislations involve removing the obligated person from the call of desires that they find pleasing to follow, and in removing them from it, there is a coercion and hardship. It is feared that these preceding attributes in the opening of the Book might lighten the burden of disobedience for those commanded to act and stir their hopes for forgiveness for their negligence, leading them to be consumed by hope and rely on what they know of Lordship and confirmed mercy, so they do not fear the consequences of neglecting obligations. Therefore, it was necessary to follow this with a mention that He is the Master on the Day of Judgment.
Thus, the description of Master or Owner was chosen here, added to the Day of Judgment. As for "Master," it indicates the establishment of justice and the absence of leniency in it, for the role of a king is to manage the welfare of the subjects and protect them, and that is why people have established kings over themselves. If it were said "Lord of the Day of Judgment," it would provide an opportunity for the corrupt to find in the nature of the Lord mercy and forgiveness. As for "Owner," it implies the establishment of recompense in the most appropriate manner for the actions that are rewarded.(22).

In addition to what we mentioned earlier, "religion" means recompense, and it encompasses reckoning, obedience, coercion, and governance, all of which are meanings of religion, and the word "religion" is more suitable for the expression.

The surah begins with ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgment﴿, and all of this is in the style of addressing with the pronoun of the absent, then it shifts to direct address, that is, with the pronoun of the present addressee, in His saying ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿. If we were to measure this against the context of the initial verses, it would have been more appropriate to say "We worship You and we seek help from You." So why did the Exalted One not say this? One of the rhetorical devices is "iltifat" (23), which is the transition of speech from addressing with the pronoun of the addressee or the absent or the speaker to another form. This alone is considered a mark of eloquence, and Ibn Jinni called it the courage of the Arabic language because this change renews the listener's engagement. If a subtle consideration that suits the transition is added, it becomes one of the rhetorical devices and is counted among the treasures by the eloquent Arabs. There are countless instances of it in the Qur'an with precise appropriateness in the transition; the general benefit of iltifat is to invigorate the listener's activity and stimulate the mind to listen and pay attention. As for the benefit required by the context, when the eloquent speaker shifts, this transition has a benefit beyond the general one. An example of this is: ﴾He it is who causes you to travel on land and sea; until when you are in the ships and they sail with them with a good wind, and they rejoice in it, there comes a stormy wind, and the waves come to them from every place, and they think they are surrounded, they call upon Allah, making religion pure for Him. Another matter regarding the shift in address here is that when a person begins to pray, he usually comes with his mind preoccupied with worldly matters. The prayer gradually leads him towards humility until he reaches the sincerity of worshiping Allah alone and the sincerity of seeking help from Allah alone. His heart has then brought to mind the meeting with his Lord, so it is fitting that he addresses Him with the pronoun of the addressee after having addressed Him with the pronoun of the absent.
Another point is to achieve the benefit of sincerity in servitude and in seeking help, and for this reason, the pronoun in both is that of the addressee.
Why did He choose the word "the path" (as-sirat) instead of "the way" or "the route"? If we observe the linguistic structure of "the path," it is on the pattern of (fa'al with a kasra on the fa), which is one of the patterns indicating comprehensiveness, like a belt, a strap, a cover, and a bedding. This form indicates comprehensiveness, unlike the word "the way," which does not convey the same meaning. "The path" indicates that it is wide and spacious, accommodating all who tread upon it, while the word "way" is on the pattern of fa'il, meaning that which is tread upon, and "route" is on the pattern of fa'il, and we say "the road has become wide" if many people walk upon it, but its form does not indicate comprehensiveness. Thus, the word "the path" indicates comprehensiveness and spaciousness, and this is in the essence of the linguistic structure. (Al-Zamakhshari said in his book Al-Kashaf that "the path" is from "sarat," as if it swallows the routes whenever those who tread upon it walk in it, as if it swallows them due to its vastness).

Interpretation of Seeking Refuge
The Lord of the worlds, Exalted is He, commanded seeking refuge when reading the Noble Qur'an, as He, the Exalted, said: ﴾So when you recite the Qur'an, seek refuge with Allah from the accursed devil (98)﴿, then afterwards the reader begins with Bismillah al-Rahman al-Rahim. So seeking refuge from the devil by saying: "I seek refuge with Allah," indicates the negation of what is not appropriate in words, actions, and beliefs, and saying: "In the name of Allah," indicates what is appropriate in words, actions, and beliefs. You will notice the style of negation before affirmation in many instances, as you say: "There is no deity," before you say: "except Allah." You negate and disavow every deity and every partner in the universe that could rise to the rank of divinity. Then after this negation, you follow it with affirmation by submitting, believing, and being certain that the only deity worthy of worship, the one who governs this entire universe, is Allah alone, without any partner. You also find this negation in Allah's statement: ﴾So whoever disbelieves in Taghut﴿, then follows it with affirmation in His statement: ﴾and believes in Allah﴿, as a sign of the necessity to abandon all methods of disbelief, polytheism, and atheism, and to disavow them before submitting, yielding, accepting, and believing in the methods of truth. For it is the right of this truth, upon which the heavens and the earth were established, that it should not be mixed with falsehood, nor should it be tainted with impurity or filth that corrupts it.

Interpretation of the Basmala
As for the Basmala, its wording is: ﴾In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿, and the letter "alif" in "bism" is omitted both in pronunciation and writing, as a simplification due to frequent usage, and it is only omitted with the divine name "Allah" and is retained in other instances, such as His saying: ﴾Read in the name of your Lord who created﴿(40).
Beginning in the name of Allah is the etiquette that Allah revealed to His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) at the very start of the Quran, by consensus, which is His saying: ﴾Read in the name of your Lord...﴿.(41)
When you begin everything in the name of Allah, it is as if you place Allah by your side to assist you. And from the mercy of Allah, Exalted is He, He taught us to begin everything in His name, for Allah is the name that encompasses all attributes of perfection, Exalted is He. Actions typically require multiple attributes; thus, when you start a task, you need Allah's power, assistance, and mercy. If Allah, Exalted is He, had not informed us of the name that encompasses all attributes, we would have to specify the attributes we need, such as saying, "In the name of Allah the Strong," "In the name of Allah the Provider," "In the name of Allah the Responsive," "In the name of Allah the Capable," "In the name of Allah the Beneficial," and so on with other names and attributes we wish to seek help from. However, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, has made us say "In the name of Allah," which encompasses all these attributes. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Every matter of importance that does not begin with 'In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful' is severed" (42).
The meaning of "severed" is that it is cut off at the tail, indicating an incomplete action with something missing (43).
The manuscripts unanimously begin with ﴾In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿, but the scholars differ regarding it. Malik said: It is not an ayah (verse) from either Al-Fatiha or any other surah except from An-Naml specifically. Al-Shafi'i said: It is an ayah only from Al-Fatiha. Ibn Abbas said: It is an ayah from every surah.
Malik's evidence is what is authentically reported from him (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "A surah has been revealed to me that is not found in the Torah, nor in the Gospel, nor in the Furqan like it," then he said: ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿, and he did not mention the Basmala. Likewise, it is also authentically reported that Allah says: "I have divided prayer between Me and My servant into two halves. The servant says: ﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿," starting with it without the Basmala.
Al-Shafi'i's evidence is what is authentically reported that "the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) used to recite ﴾In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful﴿."

A Muslim cannot differ with another Muslim on any matter of belief as long as it is definitively established, and there is no disagreement between them on its certainty. If there is a disagreement regarding its certainty or probability, then with the occurrence of the disagreement (if the disagreement is justified), it naturally descends from the level of certainty to the level of probability. Muslims have disagreed on whether the Basmala (the phrase "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful") is a verse in Al-Fatiha (the Opening) or not; thus, the mere existence of disagreement regarding the Basmala indicates that it is not a verse. Therefore, by saying "In the name of Allah," He deserved mercy from the name of the All-Merciful (al-Rahman), and by saying "All praise is due to Allah," He deserved mercy from the name of the Most Merciful (al-Rahim). Indeed, the words "the Most Merciful (al-Rahman)" and "the Most Compassionate (al-Rahim)" are derived from "mercy (rahma)", which is the source. The root of the word is "R H M" and its verb is "rahima". The active participle in this context is "the one who shows mercy (al-Rahim)" and "the Most Merciful (al-Rahman)" and "the Most Compassionate (al-Rahim)".
The term "الرحمان" (the Most Gracious), whose root is "الرحمان" (the Gracious), is built on the pattern "فَعْلاَن" (fa'lan); and this pattern, which includes an additional long alif and a nun at the end of a three-letter root word, indicates abundance, emphasis, and exaggeration. The exaggeration comes in both the giving and the permanence of the giving..
Al-Nasafi said: "The Most Merciful (al-Rahman) means, according to the people of language, the one who has mercy that has no limit thereafter, for the form 'fa'lan' is from the patterns of intensity. You say: 'A man is thirsty' if he is at the height of thirst(89). The form 'fa'lan' also conveys the meaning of occurrence, renewal, and fullness with the description. Here, in the term 'al-Rahman', it is a description that evokes fear, as the thirsty among creatures, when they drink, become quenched, and thus their thirst is removed. The angry person, when he refrains from anger, becomes pleased. Likewise, conditions are renewed for creatures. The description of 'al-Rahman' relates to humans with their varying inclinations and actions, so it has states according to its relation to those who are the recipients of that mercy. You find that 'al-Rahman' shows compassion and tenderness towards those who deserve mercy, and He is severe in punishment towards those who deserve punishment. A person becomes apprehensive if he realizes that he may do something that causes the attribute of 'al-Rahman' to change regarding him, so he may not attain that mercy. However, its foundation is based on permanence and continuity. The attributes that come in the form 'fa'lan' vary in change according to what they relate to. Joy occurs and quickly fades away, while some attributes, like hunger and satiety, take longer to fade. The foundation of 'al-Rahman' is based on permanence and continuity."
As for the term "Rahim" (رحيم), it is built on the pattern "Fa'il" (فعيل), and this pattern, that is, the addition of a long vowel (ya) between the second letter (ح) and the third letter (م) of the root indicates distinction and exaggeration, meaning that its possessor is characterized by that attribute to the extent that it has become predominant over him, similar to "Hakim" (حكيم), "Alim" (عليم), "Sami" (سميع), and "Hafiz" (حفيظ). From His saying, ﴾All praise is due to Allah﴿, we learn that Allah is alive, possessing the most beautiful attributes and excellent actions, so we praise Him for His attributes and for His actions and bounties. Praise is the expression and calling out of the beautiful, whether it be a blessing or otherwise, with love and reverence; you say: "I praised the man for his bounty," and "I praised him for his lineage and bravery." As for gratitude, it is specifically for the blessing and is expressed through the heart, tongue, and limbs. He said:

And my gratitude was not sufficient for your generosity, but I endeavored in my effort to honor you.
This means that my glorification of you was not sufficient to fulfill the right of your gift, but I intended through my striving in honoring you a means. He clarified this by saying: "Your blessings upon me are manifested through my hand, my tongue, and my heart; they are yours along with their actions." The noble master said: This is a moral testimony that gratitude applies to the actions of the three resources, and it indicates that he made them a reward for the blessing. Everything that is a reward for a blessing, by convention, is called gratitude in language. It is as if he said: "Your blessings have increased upon me, thus it is obligatory to fulfill the types of gratitude to you," and he emphasized this until he made their resources a possession for them.
Praise is by the tongue alone; it is one of the branches of gratitude, and from it is his saying (peace be upon him): "Praise is the head of gratitude; no servant has truly thanked Allah who has not praised Him" (102). He made it the head of gratitude because mentioning the blessing with the tongue and praising its giver is more widespread and indicative of its status than belief and the etiquette of the limbs. Praise is the opposite of blame, and gratitude is the opposite of ingratitude (103).
It has been mentioned in Lisan al-Arab that "praise and gratitude are close in meaning, but praise is broader than gratitude because you praise a person for their inherent qualities and for their gifts, but you do not thank them for their qualities. Therefore, the choice of praise is preferable to commendation and gratitude."

﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.﴿
The meaning of worship:
Worship (ibadah) in language means submission and humility towards others with the intention of glorifying them, and it is not permissible to do so except for Allah, and it is used in the meaning of obedience.
Ibn Manzur said: "The servant (abd): a human being, whether free or enslaved, which indicates that he is a created being under his Creator, Exalted is He. It is said that so-and-so is a clear servant of servitude. The essence of servitude is submission and humility. And worship (ibadah) is obedience; Ibn al-Anbari said: 'So-and-so is a worshipper (abid): he is the one who is submissive to his Lord, yielding and compliant to His command.'" ((133)).
Al-Firuzabadi said, "... and worship is obedience" ((134)), and based on this, the definition of worship in the Arabic language is: humiliation and submission that necessitates obedience to the one worshiped in command and prohibition. Therefore, a slave is called "abd" (عبد) because he humbles himself and submits to his master in command and prohibition regarding matters of life.
In terminology: they mentioned several similar definitions for it, including:
(1) - It is the highest rank of submission to Allah and humility before Him.
(2) - She is the one who is obligated against the desires of her own self, in reverence for her Lord.
(3) - It is an action that is intended only to glorify Allah through His command.
(4) - It is a term for what Allah loves and is pleased with among statements, actions, and both outward and inward deeds.
(5) Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) defined it as: "The perfection of love for Allah along with the perfection of submission to Allah along with the perfection of fear of Allah."

The reality of worship: it is the ultimate sanctification of the worshipped, Exalted is He, and it is the greatest manifestation of gratitude that the created being must perform towards the One who has bestowed upon him the blessing of creation and existence. It is accomplished through the submission of the heart and limbs to Allah, acting in accordance with what He has commanded, through actions intended solely for the sake of Allah, without any partner, in the manner that the worshipped, Exalted is He, has commanded, out of love and submission to Him, and out of fear of His punishment. The rulings of worship are definitive
The rulings of worship are definitive from Allah, Exalted is He, and a person performs them in the manner that Allah has commanded, as worship is merely a system that organizes the relationship between the created being and the Creator, that is, the relationship between the worshipper and the worshipped. This system cannot be established by the created being at all, because the created being does not comprehend the reality of the Creator to organize his relationship with Him, nor does he grasp His essence to know how to worship Him. It is impossible for a person to devise a system of worship in his mind that organizes his relationship with the Creator, that is, organizes his sanctification of the Creator, because establishing this organization requires understanding the reality of the Creator, which is impossible. Thus, it has become impossible for a person to formulate the rulings of worship in his mind. Hence, it is necessary for the system of worship to come from the Creator, not from the created being, that is, to come from the worshipped, not from the worshipper. Therefore, the rulings of worship must come solely from Allah, and not from humans, and humans have no role in any part of it, no matter how small, because it is impossible for them to establish it. This system must be conveyed by the Creator to the created being, so that he may worship the deity accordingly. Thus, the need for messengers to convey to people the rulings of worship is an absolute necessity, as it is impossible for people to establish rulings in worship, and because they can only come from Allah, Exalted is He. Worship can only be derived from revelation:
The purpose of worship is to refine the soul by turning to Allah, submitting to Him, and adhering to His rulings by complying with His commands. It can only be derived through revelation of both types: the Glorious Book and the Sunnah of the infallible Prophet, who does not speak out of desire. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And he does not speak out of desire. It is but a revelation revealed﴿.

﴾It is You we worship and You we ask for help﴿
The Exalted One has placed the two objects "we worship" and "we ask for help" in this order for the purpose of specification, for He alone deserves worship. Thus, He did not say "We worship You and we ask You for help" because that does not indicate the specification of worship to Allah, the Exalted. However, the phrase ﴾It is You we worship﴿ means to specify worship to Allah alone, and likewise in seeking help ﴾It is You we ask for help﴿ is exclusively to Allah. ﴾Our Lord, in You we have put our trust, and to You we turn in repentance, and to You is the final destination﴿ (Al-Mumtahanah 4) is all exclusively for Allah alone. Thus, reliance, turning in repentance, and the reference are all to Him, Exalted is He. ﴾And upon Allah, let those who would rely rely﴿ (Ibrahim 12).
Why did He repeat "You" with the verb of seeking help and did not say "It is You we worship and we ask for help"?
The repetition emphasizes the exclusivity of the one sought for help. If we had limited ourselves to one pronoun, "It is You we worship and we ask for help," it would not have indicated the one sought for help but rather the one worshipped only. If we had limited ourselves to one pronoun, it would have been understood that one cannot draw near to Him except by combining worship and seeking help, meaning that one does not worship without seeking help nor seeks help without worship, which is not the case. Rather, we worship Him independently and seek help from Him independently, and they may coincide; thus, the repetition in the pronoun ﴾It is You we worship and It is You we ask for help﴿ is necessary. Additionally, repetition serves as an emphasis in language; repetition carries strength and affirmation for seeking help in what is not omitted.
﴾It is You we worship and It is You we ask for help﴿: The Exalted One has released the verb of seeking help without specifying "we ask for help for something" or "we ask for help for obedience" or anything else, but He has made it general to include everything and not limited to one matter of the worldly affairs. It encompasses everything a person wants to seek help from his Lord regarding, for seeking help is not restricted to a specific matter. The Exalted One expressed seeking help and worship using the plural pronoun (we worship and we ask for help) rather than the singular expression "I worship and I ask for help," and this indicates the importance of community in Islam; thus, reading is required.

Faith between reason, innate disposition, and conscience:
﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿: Indeed, belief in the Creator and Sustainer is innate in every human being, but this innate belief comes through intuition, which is an uncertain path and does not lead to a solid foundation if left alone. Intuition often attributes to what one believes things that have no basis in reality, yet intuition imagines them as necessary qualities of what one believes, leading to disbelief or misguidance.
Idolatry and superstitions are nothing but the result of a flawed conscience, and for this reason, Islam did not leave the conscience alone as a means of faith, so that it does not attribute to Allah characteristics that contradict divinity, or make Him possible to be embodied in material things, or conceive the possibility of drawing near to Him by worshiping material objects, which would lead either to disbelief or polytheism, or to illusions and superstitions that true faith rejects.
Therefore, Islam has mandated the use of reason alongside intuition, and it has made it obligatory for a Muslim to use their intellect when believing in Allah, Exalted is He. It has prohibited blind imitation in matters of faith, thus establishing reason as a judge in belief in Allah, Exalted is He. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for those of understanding﴿. Thus, it is obligatory for every Muslim to ensure that their faith arises from thought, inquiry, and reflection, and to allow reason to have absolute authority in belief in Allah, Exalted is He. The call to contemplate the universe to derive its laws and to be guided to faith in its Creator is repeated by the Qur'an hundreds of times in its various chapters, all directed at the rational faculties of human beings, urging them to ponder and reflect so that their faith is based on reason and evidence, and warning them against accepting what they found their forefathers upon without examination and scrutiny and self-confidence.

And although it is necessary for a person to use reason to arrive at faith in Allah, the Exalted, he cannot comprehend what is beyond his senses and beyond his intellect, because human reason is limited, and its power, no matter how elevated and developed, is confined by boundaries it cannot surpass. Guidance and misguidance:
Guidance in language means righteousness and indication; it is said, "He guided him to the religion," he guides him guidance, and "I guided you to the path and the house" means I made it known to you. Misguidance is the opposite of righteousness. Guidance in Sharia is the guidance to Islam and belief in it, and misguidance in Sharia is the deviation from Islam, as indicated by His saying:


[Page 56]

Peace be upon him: "My community will never unite upon misguidance." Narrated by Ahmad. Allah has made Paradise for the guided and Hellfire for the misguided, meaning that Allah rewards the guided and punishes the misguided. The connection of reward or punishment to guidance and misguidance indicates that guidance and misguidance are actions of the human being and not from Allah. If they were from Allah, He would not reward for guidance and punish for misguidance, as this would attribute injustice to Allah, the Exalted. When He punishes someone whom He Himself has misguided, He would be unjust to him, and Exalted is Allah above that. He, the Exalted, said: ﴾And your Lord is not unjust to the servants (46)﴿ and He said: ﴾And I am not unjust to the servants (29)﴿.
Guidance and misguidance between compulsion and choice
As for guidance and misguidance, there is no doubt that it is the human being who is guided and who goes astray. If he is guided, he has attained guidance by himself through his intellect and thinking, in his striving and actions. He is guided by choice and goes astray by choice, without any compulsion, just like any action among the actions that he controls. Thus, guidance and misguidance are not among the actions that occur upon a person without his control, such as the actions that belong to what we call the system of existence, nor are they among the actions that are beyond the capacity of the human being to avert, nor among the actions necessitated by the system of existence.(145) Therefore, they are not from the sphere that controls him, but rather from the sphere that he controls.
Guidance and misguidance are not like the beating of the heart, the color of the eyes, the shape of the head, and similar things that are part of the system of existence. They are not like a sleeping person falling from a roof, or a careless person slipping on something smooth, nor like the disbelief of a weak person threatened with death by a strong one, nor anything similar that is beyond the human being's capacity to avert. Likewise, they are not like the wings of a bird, or the legs of a walker, or light for the eyes, or anything similar that is necessitated by the system of existence. Rather, they are like eating by a human being, like traveling, walking, buying and selling, fasting and praying, and other actions that fall within the sphere that the human being controls, where he can, by his own choice, engage in these actions or refrain from them.
Thus, guidance and misguidance are from the human being himself; they are actions of his, like all his voluntary actions.

These actions fall within the realm of destiny and divine decree, for which those upon whom these actions occur are neither rewarded nor punished. As for the actions that occur in the sphere that he controls, such as praying or not praying, selling or not selling, these are actions that the human being controls and is rewarded for or punished for, as they are outside the realm of destiny and divine decree. These verses are explicit in indicating that guidance is attributed to the servant, and that he is the one who is guided, and he is the one who goes astray. This clearly means that it is the human being who is guided, and it is he who has gone astray.
And also, indeed Allah, Exalted is He, holds people accountable; He rewards the guided and punishes the misguided, and He has arranged the reckoning based on a person's deeds. He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Whoever does good, it is for his own soul; and whoever does evil, it is against it; and your Lord is not unjust to the servants﴿ (41) (Fussilat), ﴾So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it (7) and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it (8)﴿ (Al-Zalzalah), ﴾And whoever does righteous deeds while he is a believer, he will not fear injustice or deprivation (112)﴿ (Ta-Ha), ﴾Whoever does evil will be recompensed for it﴿ (An-Nisa 123), ﴾Allah has promised the hypocrites and the hypocritical women and the disbelievers the fire of Hell, abiding therein﴿ (At-Tawbah 68). These verses are explicit in indicating that Allah holds people accountable for guidance and misguidance, and it would be unjust if He compelled someone to do something and then held them accountable for it; Allah is far above that, Exalted is He. This is because the reckoning is for everything they did not do themselves, and because punishing individuals by Allah for an act they performed, not for what others did, is undoubtedly injustice, and Allah is far above that, Exalted is He. For this reason, it is the human being who is guided and the one who goes astray, and holding him accountable for that is just, because it is accountability for an act he himself performed, and Allah's punishment of the misguided is just, because it is punishment for their own actions.


[Page 58]

These three pieces of evidence: the reality of guidance and misguidance, the definitive verses indicating that it is the human who goes astray, and God's accountability for guidance and misguidance and His punishment of the misguided, all clearly indicate that it is the human who is guided and who goes astray. There is no doubt that guidance and misguidance are indeed actions of the human for which he is held accountable and which he is not compelled to undertake.
The attribution of guidance and misguidance to the Creator, Exalted is He, in the Qur'an:
As for the verses that indicate the attribution of guidance and misguidance, and the definitive verses that indicate that Allah is the One who created guidance and misguidance from nothing, it does not mean that Allah is the One who directly performs the act of guidance. His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾Say, "Allah guides to the truth"﴿, ﴾And they said, "Praise be to Allah, who has guided us to this; and we would not have been guided if Allah had not guided us"﴿, ﴾Whomever Allah guides, he is the rightly guided; and whomever He leads astray, you will never find for him a guiding ally﴿, ﴾And if it had been great, except for those whom Allah has guided﴿, ﴾And to glorify Allah for what He has guided you﴿, ﴾So among them are those whom Allah has guided, and among them are those upon whom misguidance has been justified﴿, ﴾And He found you lost and guided﴿, and other verses do not mean that Allah directly performed the act of guidance, but rather that Allah created guidance. It cannot be said that guidance is attributed to Allah in these verses as a true attribution, meaning that Allah is the One who performed the guidance; this cannot be said because there is an indication that shifts the attribution from the truth to metaphor, that is, it shifts the attribution from action to creation. This indication is the verses that attribute guidance to the human being himself, such as His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾So whoever is guided, he is only guided for himself; and whoever goes astray, say, "Indeed, I am only one of the warners"﴿, and the verses that attribute misguidance to people, to jinn, and to Satan, such as His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾Our Lord, show us those who led us astray from among jinn and men; we will put them beneath our feet so they become among the lowest﴿, ﴾And who is more unjust than one who invents a lie against Allah to mislead the people without knowledge?﴿, ﴾And none led us astray except the criminals﴿, ﴾Our Lord, these are the ones who led us astray; so give them a double punishment from the Fire﴿, ﴾A faction of the People of the Book wished to mislead you, but they do not mislead except themselves, and they do not perceive﴿, ﴾And they said, "Our Lord, indeed we have obeyed our masters and our dignitaries, and they led us astray from the way"﴿, ﴾And among the people are those who argue about Allah without knowledge and follow every rebellious devil﴿, ﴾It has been decreed upon him that whoever takes him as an ally, he will mislead him and guide him to the punishment of the Blaze﴿, ﴾Indeed, those who go astray from the path of Allah will have a severe punishment for what they forgot on the Day of Reckoning﴿.
To other verses, they all attribute guidance and misguidance to others than Allah. When compared with the verses that attribute guidance and misguidance to Allah, it becomes evident that the intended meaning of those verses is that guidance diverts them from their original meaning, that is, from the action.


[Page 59]

And it should not be said that the verses in which the action is attributed to Allah are the evidence that the attribution of guidance to others than Allah is metaphorical and not literal. This cannot be said, because the verses in which guidance and misguidance are attributed to the servant are accompanied by a ruling regarding the guidance and misguidance of the servant in a literal sense. Moreover, in the verses where guidance and misguidance are attributed to Allah, there is no evidence indicating that this attribution is literal. Thus, the verses that are accompanied by the evidence of reward and punishment for the guidance or misguidance of the servant serve as the evidence that the verses attributing guidance and misguidance to Allah are metaphorical and not literal, meaning they indicate that Allah created guidance and misguidance, but did not directly perform them.
Guidance and misguidance, will and desire:
As for the verses that associate guidance and misguidance with the will and decree of Allah, their meaning is that no one is guided by compulsion from Allah, nor is anyone led astray by compulsion from Allah, and it does not mean that the will of Allah is what causes guidance. Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Indeed, Allah leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills﴿ (Fatir 8) ﴾And Allah leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills﴿ (Ibrahim 4) ﴾But He leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills﴿ (An-Nahl 93), ﴾Whomever Allah wills to guide, He expands his breast to Islam, and whomever He wills to lead astray, He makes his breast constricted and tight, as if he were climbing into the sky. Thus Allah places defilement upon those who do not believe﴿ (Al-An'am 125), ﴾Whomever Allah wills to lead astray, He lets him go astray, and whomever He wills, He places him on a straight path﴿ (Al-An'am 39) and other verses that associate guidance and misguidance with will and decree. These do not mean that will and decree are the cause of guidance, or that they are what brings about guidance, but rather they are like any action of the servants that is associated with will and decree. It is like the saying of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him): "Whomever Allah intends good for, He grants him understanding in religion" (Bukhari and Muslim). In fact, every action of a human being is only performed by the will and decree of Allah, and not just guidance and misguidance. Therefore, these verses do not serve as evidence that guidance and misguidance are not from the human being, but rather as evidence that a person is neither guided nor led astray by compulsion from Allah.
And as for the verses that state that certain people do not find guidance, they are of two types: the first type consists of verses that inform about past people, recounting their story that they did not find guidance, and the second type consists of verses that mention specific attributes of people and state that those who possess these attributes do not find guidance as long as they are characterized by these attributes. As for the first type, it is like His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾Indeed, those who disbelieve, it is all the same for them whether you warn them or do not warn them, they will not believe. (6) Allah has sealed their hearts and upon their hearing and upon their sight is a veil, and for them is a great punishment﴿ (Al-Baqarah), ﴾No! Rather, what they used to earn has covered their hearts﴿ (14) (Al-Mutaffifin), ﴾And it was revealed to Noah that no one from your people will believe except for those who have already believed﴿ (Hud 36), and other such verses.


[Page 60]

The verses are a revelation from Allah to His prophets about specific people who will not believe, and this falls within the knowledge of Allah. It does not mean that there is a group that believes and a group that does not believe; it relates to faith and is a report about a past reality. There is no indication in it that there are people who cannot guide themselves. The distinction is clear between Allah knowing in His prior knowledge that so-and-so will not believe, meaning that so-and-so will not choose guidance, and this being different from compelling him not to believe. The fact that Allah, Exalted is He, knows in advance does not indicate any form of coercion. The knowledge of Allah is not like human knowledge, which occurs after the known event takes place. Just as He, Blessed and Exalted, encompasses the hidden aspects of the human soul and knows what it will choose at every place and time, this knowledge is in no way a form of coercion(146).
As for the second category, it is like His saying: ﴾And Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people﴿, ﴾And Allah does not guide the disobedient people﴿, ﴾And Allah does not guide the disbelieving people﴿, ﴾Indeed, Allah does not guide the one who is a transgressor and a liar﴿, and other verses. These verses mention specific attributes; a person cannot be guided as long as they possess these attributes. As long as a person is characterized by wrongdoing, disobedience, disbelief, or transgression—such as spending money on prohibited things, or lying—they will not be guided and will not find guidance as long as they are characterized by these traits. If they abandon them, then they may be guided, because being characterized by them contradicts guidance. This is the meaning of the verses, and it does not imply that there are people who cannot be guided. Every person can be guided and can go astray; there are no people who cannot be guided, nor are there people who cannot go astray.
As for His saying: ﴾If you strive for their guidance, then indeed, Allah does not guide whom He leads astray﴿, this means He does not guide the one He knows will go astray. It is a report about the knowledge of Allah, like any predetermined action upon a person, meaning it is written in the preserved tablet by the knowledge of Allah that this person will choose misguidance. In this case, the person does not deserve to have the means of success for guidance caused for them due to their following the path of misguidance. If the potential for guidance stirs within them, Allah will prepare for them the means of guidance.
The act of leading astray relates to the issue of will and decree, which differs from the matter of commanding to go astray or compelling towards misguidance. Failing to differentiate between what is commanded and what is intended leads to the error of confusing human coercion and choice, and that Allah's knowledge encompasses everything in the universe by His will, not by coercion. The will does not necessarily entail that the action will occur in a manner pleasing to the one who wills it, because if He wills but does not compel, and if the matter occurs contrary to what pleases Him, that is evidence that He did not.

The difference between the creation of guidance and the facilitation of it:
As we have mentioned, the attribution of guidance to Allah, Blessed and Exalted, in those verses may either indicate the creation of guidance or the facilitation of guidance. Whenever it is attributed to the servant, it means the direct act of guidance and bearing responsibility for his actions.
The facilitation of guidance is from Allah, and it does not mean the creation of guidance within the human being. For the creation of guidance is the establishment of the capacity for guidance, while the facilitation of guidance is the preparation of the means for this guidance for the human being, which is different from the matter of creating guidance from nothing within the human being. There is a difference between creating guidance from nothing and preparing the means for this guidance.
Thus, facilitation pertains to the means, that is, the means of this guidance, or the qualities of this guidance, which, if a person possesses them, he will be guided. As for creation, it pertains to bringing this guidance into existence from nothing within the human being, and its specification according to his type with the capacity for guidance he possesses. Creation is the bringing into existence of the human being with guidance within him, meaning he is capable of this guidance whenever he wishes, for he is created with this guidance, just as he is created with the capacity for speech, thought, and the capacity for going astray and being misled.
Allah has created guidance in the human being just as He has created misguidance. He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And We guided him to the two paths﴿, meaning He placed within him good and evil, that is, He created within him good and evil. However, it is the human being who directly engages with what Allah has created within him of the capacity for guidance and misguidance. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And the soul and He who fashioned it (7) and inspired it with its wickedness﴿.

If he possesses the attributes of misguidance, such as disbelief or committing sins and prohibitions, then these attributes contradict guidance, and he will not be guided and cannot be guided. However, if he possesses the attributes of guidance, such as being guided to Islam and believing in it, following its commands, and avoiding its prohibitions, then these attributes contradict misguidance, and he must be guided, and he must continue on the path of guidance. But if he deviates from the path of guidance by adopting some attributes of misguidance, then he will go astray and will not be guided unless he abandons that action which led him to misguidance.

And He uses "guided him to it" to mean He made it clear to him, and guidance is on stages and not a single guidance. The one far from the path, the misguided, needs someone to bring him to it and show him the way (we use "guided him to it").
And whoever reaches the path needs a guide who knows the conditions of the path and the places of safety, salvation, and destruction for self-confidence. Then, if he traverses the path, in the end, he needs someone to show him his goal: we use the transitive verb by itself.
And finally: He, Exalted is He, used the lam: ﴾And they said, "Praise be to Allah, who has guided us to this, and we would not have been guided if Allah had not guided us"﴿ (al-A'raf 43). And this is the conclusion of the guidance.
We summarize what has been mentioned above as follows:
A person who is far away needs someone to guide him to the path; we use the transitive verb with "to".
- If he arrives and needs someone to inform him of the way and his conditions, we use the transitive verb by itself.
If one takes the path and needs someone to convey his intention, we use the transitive verb with "to".
Guidance with the preposition "lam" has never been used with "the path" (as-sabeel) or "the straight path" (as-sirat) in the Qur'an because "the straight path" is not an end but rather a means that leads to the end, and "lam" is only used when referring to the end. Allah, Exalted is He, has reserved guidance with "lam" for Himself alone or for the Qur'an because it is the culmination of all forms of guidance, as in His saying ﴾Indeed, this Qur'an guides to that which is most upright﴿ and His saying ﴾Allah guides to His light whom He wills﴿.


[Page 67]

We return to the verse ﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿ (the verb is transitive by itself). Here, this transitive verb is used to encompass several meanings: for the one who has deviated from the path, we ask Allah, Exalted is He, to guide him to it; and for the one who is on the path, we ask Allah, Exalted is He, to make him aware of the conditions of the path and to grant him steadfastness and firmness upon the path.
Here another question arises, and we say, as previously mentioned, Exalted is He presented the two objects of worship and seeking help in ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿. So why did He not say, "Guide us"? This meaning is not correct, for the precedence of ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿ restricts the exclusivity, and it is not permissible to say "Guide us," meaning "specifically guide us and do not guide anyone else." This is not permissible, so the precedence here is not correct. The meaning requires precedence in assistance and seeking help, but it does not require it in guidance; hence He said ﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿.
Why did He say "Guide us" and not "Guide me"?
Because it is suitable for the context of the previous verses, and just as in the verses regarding seeking help and worship, it necessitated the gathering in guidance as well.
And in it is a promotion of the spirit of community and a killing of the spirit of selfishness and individualism. It involves removing selfishness and exclusivity from the self by supplicating for others as we supplicate for ourselves.
Gathering upon guidance and the group’s journey on the straight path is a sign of strength. When the travelers increase, the sense of companionship grows and steadfastness strengthens. A solitary traveler may weaken, become weary, fall, or be devoured by wolves. Thus, the more the travelers increase, the more it invites reassurance and comfort.
And gathering is a mercy, while division is a torment. Allah, Exalted is He, refers to the matter of gathering and the comfort found in it, as well as the nature of the self's love for gathering, as mentioned in His noble saying ﴾And whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger, He will admit him to gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein forever, and that is the great success (13)﴿ (An-Nisa). The term "abiding" is in the plural form because the believers in Paradise enjoy the companionship of one another. And His saying ﴾And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger and transgresses His limits, He will admit him to a Fire, abiding therein forever, and he will have a humiliating punishment (14)﴿ (An-Nisa) indicates that the torment includes not only the punishment of the disbeliever but also the torment of solitude, as if Allah, Exalted is He, has punished him with two things: the Fire and solitude.
Therefore, when He, Exalted is He, said ﴾Guide us to the straight path﴿, there is something of reassurance and comfort in this supplication, which is linked to the beginning, the middle, and the end of the surah.
﴾All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds﴿ The mission of the Lord is guidance, and guidance is often associated with the name of the Lord, as it is linked to the Lord of the worlds and to His saying ﴾The Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate﴿ because whoever Allah guides, He has indeed shown mercy to. And now you are asking the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate for guidance, meaning you are asking the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate not to leave you misguided and without guidance. Then He said ﴾You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help﴿ so worship cannot be realized except by following the straight path.


[Page 68]

And likewise, seeking assistance includes asking for guidance to the straight path ﴾the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor﴿, meaning the path of those who have followed the straight path, ﴾not of those who have evoked [Your] anger﴿, and the misguided are those who have taken a path other than the straight path. Thus, guidance and misguidance are opposites, and the misguided are the opposite of those who have followed the straight path.

﴾the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor﴿
﴾the path﴿ is a substitute for the first (meaning the straight path) - a complete substitution - and it is in the position of reiterating the action in that it is the intended reference. Its benefit is: the emphasis and specification that the path of the Muslims is the one that is affirmed to be upright, in the most certain and eloquent manner, because it is made like an explanation and clarification of it, as if it were from the evident that has no ambiguity in it, and that the straight path is what constitutes the way of the believers, and ﴾not of those who have evoked [Your] anger﴿ is a substitute for ﴾those﴿ in the sense that those who have been bestowed favor are those who are safe from anger and misguidance.
Or it is a description that clarifies or restricts in the sense that they combine between the absolute favor, which is the favor of faith, and safety from anger and misguidance, (149)
Ibn Ashur said: The intended meaning of those who have been bestowed favor is those upon whom complete blessings have been poured, and the full correspondence between those who have been bestowed favor and the guided ones is not hidden, thus in the substitution of ﴾the path of those﴿ from ﴾the straight path﴿ there is a beautiful meaning, which is that guidance is a blessing and that those who have been favored with complete blessings have been guided to the straight path. And those whom Allah has favored are the best of the previous nations from the messengers and prophets who have received complete blessings. (150) And those who follow them in righteousness until the Day of Judgment, and excellent are those as companions.
And ﴾those upon whom You have bestowed favor﴿ are those mentioned in Surah An-Nisa, where it says: ﴾And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger - those will be with the ones upon whom Allah has bestowed favor, among the prophets, the steadfast, the martyrs, and the righteous. And excellent are those as companions. * That is the bounty from Allah, and sufficient is Allah as Knower﴿ [An-Nisa: 69, 70].

Why did He say ﴾the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked [Your] anger and not of the misguided﴿?
That is, why did He express those who have been favored using the verb ﴾You have bestowed favor﴿ while the angered and the misguided are expressed with the noun?

The name indicates comprehensiveness and encompasses all times, referring to those who have incurred wrath, and signifies permanence. As for the verb, it indicates renewal and occurrence; thus, describing them as those who have incurred wrath and as misguided is evidence of permanence and continuity.
The word "ni'ma" (نعمة) with a kasra and with a fatha is derived from "na'im" (نعيم), which means comfort in living and luxury. Al-Jawhari said in his dictionary: "Ni'ma" is the hand, the favor, the blessing, and what has been bestowed upon you. Likewise, "nu'ma" (النُعْمى) follows the same pattern. If you open the noon, you extend it and say "na'ma" (النَعْماء). "Na'im" (النَعيم) is similar. A person is said to be "wasi' al-ni'ma" (واسعُ النِعْمَةِ), meaning he is wealthy.
So why did He not say "the favored ones" to indicate permanence?
If the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor were mentioned by name, the meaning would not be clear as to who has bestowed the favor; rather, the Bestower (al-Mun'im) is clarified in His saying ﴾You have bestowed favor upon them﴿ because knowing the Bestower is important. The blessings are measured by the extent of the Bestower (al-Mun'im), so the Exalted, may He be glorified, wanted to clarify the Bestower to indicate the magnitude and greatness of the blessing. It is customary in the Qur'an to attribute goodness to Allah, the Exalted, as well as blessings and favors, while He is free from attributing evil to Himself ﴾And we do not know whether evil is intended for those on earth or whether their Lord intends for them guidance (10)﴿ (al-Jinn). And Allah, the Exalted, does not attribute evil to Himself; He may say ﴾Indeed, those who do not believe in the Hereafter, We have adorned for them their deeds, so they wander blindly (4)﴿ (an-Naml), but He does not say, "We adorned for them the evil of their deeds" ﴾The evil of their deeds was adorned for them﴿ (at-Tawbah, verse 37) ﴾The love of desires was adorned for people﴿ (Al-Imran, verse 14) ﴾The evil of his deeds was adorned for Pharaoh, and he was barred from the path﴿ (Ghafir, verse 37) ﴾Like one for whom the evil of his deeds was adorned, and they followed their desires﴿. As for the blessing, Allah, the Exalted, attributes it to Himself because all blessings are good ﴾He said, My Lord, because You have bestowed favor upon me﴿ (al-Qasas, verse 17) ﴾He is but a servant upon whom We have bestowed favor﴿ (az-Zukhruf, verse 59) ﴾And when We have bestowed favor upon man, he turns away and distances himself, but when evil touches him, he is in despair (83)﴿ (al-Isra, verse 83). And the Exalted has not attributed the blessing to anyone else except in one verse ﴾And when you said to the one whom Allah had bestowed favor upon and you had bestowed favor upon﴿ (al-Ahzab, verse 37), which is a specific blessing after Allah's favor upon him.
﴾Not the path of those who have earned Your anger, nor of those who go astray.﴿
Ibn Ashur said in the interpretation of anger: "The anger related to those who have incurred wrath is the anger of Allah. The reality of the anger known among people is a state that affects the soul, followed by a movement of the spirit outward and its agitation, leading to a desire for revenge. This state is a cause for the desire for revenge, and the desire for revenge is a cause for the occurrence of revenge. What appears to me is that the desire for revenge is not an inherent necessity of the essence of anger such that it cannot be separated from it, but it may be one of its effects. Anger is a state of the soul that arises from the occurrence of something that is not suitable for it, resulting in a dislike for the action that has incurred wrath and a dislike for its doer. It is accompanied by aversion to the one who has incurred wrath and treatment of him with violence, cutting off kindness, and causing harm, which may lead to a desire for..."


[Page 70]

Revenge against him varies, as the limit that arises in the soul during anger differs according to the varying capacities of souls to endure provocations and the differing customs in considering its causes. Perhaps those who made the desire for revenge a necessary condition for anger based their views on Arab traditions.
And since the reality of anger is impossible to attribute to Allah, Exalted is He, in its true sense due to the definitive evidence indicating His transcendence from inherent and accidental changes, it is incumbent upon the believer to divert the attribution of anger to Allah from its literal meaning(151). The method of the people of knowledge and reflection in this diversion is to interpret the term metaphorically through a necessary relationship or to use a euphemism that implies the necessary meaning. Thus, what is a characteristic of Allah from the meaning of anger is its necessary consequence, namely punishment and humiliation on the Day of Judgment and curse, which means being distanced from the people of faith and righteousness in this world, or it is of the nature of representation(152).

﴾The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have evoked [Your] anger, nor of those who are astray (7)﴿ Why is "those who have evoked [Your] anger" mentioned in the passive form while "those who are astray" is mentioned in the active participle form?
First, both are brought as nouns ﴾the ones who have incurred wrath﴿ and ﴾the misguided﴿ to indicate permanence; the wrath upon them is established, and the misguidance within them is established, with no hope for good or guidance from them. It does not say "the path of those who have incurred wrath and have gone astray," but rather ﴾the ones who have incurred wrath and the misguided﴿. Thus, both descriptions are presented in the nominal form to indicate the permanence of these two attributes in them. The question remains: why is "the ones who have incurred wrath" in the passive participle form and not "the angry" in the active participle form? "The ones who have incurred wrath" in the passive participle form means that wrath has fallen upon them, without mentioning the entity that has incurred wrath upon them, so that the wrath encompasses them from all sides—God's wrath and the wrath of those who are angry for God's sake from the angels and others. It is not specific to a particular angry entity; it is rather "the ones who have incurred wrath" from all directions. Indeed, these will be the ones upon whom their closest friends will be angry in the Hereafter ﴾And we do not see with you your intercessors whom you claimed were partners with you. Indeed, the ties between you have been severed, and what you used to claim has abandoned you (94)﴿ Al-An'am ﴾Then on the Day of Resurrection, some of you will disbelieve in others, and some of you will curse others (25)﴿ Al-Ankabut. Thus, "the ones who have incurred wrath" are from all sides, and the omission of the entity that is angry indicates generality and comprehensiveness. As for "the misguided," they are those who have gone astray.
So why was the path of those who have incurred wrath (al-maghdubi 'alayhim) mentioned before that of those who have gone astray (al-dallin)?

The ones who have incurred wrath are those who recognized their Lord and then deviated from the truth, and they are farthest away because knowledge is not like ignorance. Thus, He began with those who have incurred wrath. In the authentic hadith, it is stated that those who have incurred wrath are the Jews, while the Christians are the misguided. The Jews preceded the Christians, which is why they were mentioned first, necessitating this order.
The characteristic of those who have incurred wrath is the first sin that appeared in existence, which is the characteristic of Iblis (Satan) when he was commanded to prostrate to Adam (peace be upon him) while knowing the truth, yet he disobeyed Allah, Exalted is He. It is also the first sin that appeared on earth when the son of Adam killed his brother. Thus, it is the first sin in the higher realm and on earth ﴾And whoever kills a believer intentionally, his recompense is Hell, wherein he will abide eternally, and Allah will be angry with him and curse him and prepare for him a great punishment (93)﴿ (An-Nisa). Therefore, it was mentioned first.
As for placing those who have incurred wrath alongside those who have been blessed, it is because those who have incurred wrath are the opposite of those who have been blessed, and wrath is the opposite of blessings.
Who among us, no matter how eloquent and articulate, can produce words with such precision and balance, with beautiful structure and careful selection? Every word is a precious gem, meticulously chosen to convey profound meanings with simple words. Each one is a link in a chain that cannot be replaced, moved, brought forward, or delayed; otherwise, the meaning would be distorted, weakened, or some of its meanings would be lost.
It is the divine miracle that manifests throughout the universe and encompasses the entire Qur'an, in its entirety and in its details, its words and letters, its meanings and secrets, to be the light that Allah intended to guide and bring happiness to everyone who has a heart or listens attentively while being present (153).
And praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds.
Those who have incurred wrath and the misguided include the factions of disbelief, immorality, and disobedience. The ones who have incurred wrath are a category of factions that deliberately took this path and disregarded the religion either intentionally or through a very far-fetched interpretation, while the misguided are a category of factions that misunderstood the religion due to poor comprehension and lack of attention. Both groups are blameworthy because we are commanded to follow the path of truth and direct our efforts towards achieving it. The Jews belong to the first group, and the Christians belong to the second group.
